## Abstract

**Problem.** A one-lane branch permutation acts on five source lineages before
a marked strict exit. Pair-level Safe-Hit determines which two labels may
fuse, but a complete raw frontier must also determine the placement of the
three surviving labels and retain the terminal phase used by the same
witness.

**Approach.** We first pass from labelled injections to the 24 oriented cyclic
orders of five labels. The branch order law is a diagonal $S_5$-invariant
relation and is therefore encoded exactly by a subset of the eight double
cosets $C_5\backslash S_5/C_5$. We then use the globally enabled label-zero
return and order-fiber saturation to analyze every formal terminal placement.

**Results.** The eight-pattern order law generates a lineage-order subgroup
$K_a^{\rm ord}\le S_5$, and the reachable orders form the left-coset space
$K_a^{\rm ord}/C_5$. The terminal phase fiber then collapses: a placement
whose cyclic order is reachable admits every missing coordinate as a terminal
hole, while an unreachable order admits none. Thus every attainable placement
has exactly $n-5$ phase witnesses. For a fused pair $F$, let
$m_a(F)\in\{0,\ldots,6\}$ be the number of compatible linear orders of the
three survivors. Then

$$
 |\mathfrak S_a(F)|=m_a(F)\binom{n-2}{3},
$$

and the corresponding product parametrization gives the complete normalized
raw strict-exit frontier. Equal eight-pattern signatures therefore imply
equal phase fibers and equal source-addressed survivor spectra.

**Boundary.** The theorem is one-lane, unbudgeted, and raw. It does not solve
the multi-lane same-witness mobility obstruction, classify shortest terminal
words, or cross the typed transfer and projectability boundary.

**Keywords.** synchronizing automata; single-defect automata; packet
lineages; cyclic orders; terminal phase; survivor incidence; double cosets;
same-witness descent

## Introduction

The raw first-exit frontier of five labelled packets has two layers. The
first records which pair of source lineages occupies the terminal kernel pair.
The second records where the other three lineages land. The first is a
spectator-safe pair-hitting problem. The second is a complete incidence
problem and cannot be reconstructed from a separately chosen endpoint.

Paper XXIX classifies the complete canonical raw frontier. Papers XXX and
XXXI normalize a general rank-$(n-1)$ defect into a marked collapse and a free
branch permutation. Papers XXXII and XXXIII classify one-lane pair hitting
and expose the same-witness obstruction in multiple lanes. Paper XXXIV then
shows, within the one-lane dihedral family, that equal fused-pair data can
carry different survivor spectra [@paper29; @paper30; @paper31; @paper32;
@paper33; @paper34].

This paper gives the complete one-lane arbitrary-branch answer, beginning
with a finite order law. Let

$$
 \mathfrak C_5=\operatorname{CycOrd}(T_0),
 \qquad
 |\mathfrak C_5|=24.
 \tag{1.1}
$$

For a branch permutation $a$, the relation $R_a$ records which cyclic order
can be changed into which other order by one application of $a$. Simultaneous
source-label relabelling makes this relation diagonal $S_5$-invariant. It is
therefore encoded exactly by a subset of the eight relative patterns in

$$
 C_5\backslash S_5/C_5.
 \tag{1.2}
$$

The key step is a terminal-phase collapse. The label-zero return implements
$a$ globally, and a finite positive power implements $a^{-1}$. Hence the
reachable injection set is $a$-invariant. Once a formal terminal placement
has a reachable cyclic order, every one of its holes can be used as the
deleted coordinate of a genuine terminal witness. Terminal phase is still
part of the witness, but it contributes no additional acceptance condition.

This yields the completion theorem of the paper. For each fused pair, the reachable
cyclic orders select between zero and six linear orders of the survivor
labels. Each selected order may be placed on any three coordinates outside
the kernel pair. The frontier is therefore a direct product of an order set
and a coordinate subset set.

The three principal results are:

$$
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \textbf{Theorem A:}\quad
 &R_a\longleftrightarrow\mathcal P_5(a),\\
 &\Omega_a^{\rm reach}
   =\operatorname{Reach}_{R_a}(\omega_0)
   \cong K_a^{\rm ord}/H;\\[1mm]
 \textbf{Theorem B:}\quad
 &\Phi_a(\mu)
   =Q\setminus\mu(T_0)\\
 &\quad\Longleftrightarrow
   \operatorname{ord}_Q(\mu)\in\Omega_a^{\rm reach};\\[1mm]
 \textbf{Theorem C:}\quad
 &\mathfrak S_a(\lambda_0;F)
   \cong M_a(F)\times
   \binom{Q\setminus\{0,1\}}{3}.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{1.3}
$$

Here $K_a^{\rm ord}/H$ is a left-coset space, not a quotient group, and
$\binom{Q\setminus\{0,1\}}{3}$ denotes the set of three-element subsets of
$Q\setminus\{0,1\}$.

In Theorem B the phase fiber is empty when the displayed order condition
fails. Theorem C is the complete one-lane survivor-frontier classification:
all branch-dependent information is carried by the finite set $M_a(F)$, while
the coordinate choice is free.

The argument proceeds as follows:

1. Section 2 states the normalized branch system and the inherited
   order-fiber saturation theorem.
2. Section 3 proves Theorem A: the 24-state order reduction, eight-pattern
   compression, and lineage-order subgroup description.
3. Section 4 proves Theorem B: terminal-phase collapse and the same-witness
   terminal criterion.
4. Section 5 proves Theorem C: the complete survivor-spectrum and raw-frontier
   classification.
5. Section 6 records controls, scope, and the raw-to-typed firewall.

### Related Work and Novelty Boundary

The Černý conjecture and the general theory of synchronizing automata concern
short reset words [@cerny1964; @volkov2008; @volkov2022survey]. Circular,
almost-group, and completely reachable automata provide neighboring
structural settings [@dubuc1998; @berlinkovNicaud2020; @casasTorres2024;
@don2016; @gonzeJungers2018; @ferensSzykula2026]. This paper proves neither a
reset bound nor a complete-reachability characterization.

Subset synchronization, monotonicity, and dynamic word constraints concern
related partial and order-sensitive reachability problems
[@eppstein1990; @ryzhikovShemyakov2018; @wolf2020]. Here every intermediate
step must keep five source-labelled lineages injective, and the output retains
the incidence of every source label at one declared strict exit.

Within the RIME line, the new claims are an exact eight-pattern order law for
terminal incidence, an all-$n$ terminal-phase collapse for arbitrary one-lane
branch permutations, and a complete source-addressed survivor formula. The
result extends the dihedral orientation classification of Paper XXXIV but does
not reopen its pair-level non-descent theorem.

## The Normalized One-Lane System

Fix

$$
 n\ge6,
 \qquad
 Q=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,
 \qquad
 E=Q\setminus\{0\},
 \tag{2.1}
$$

with $p(q)=q+1$. Let

$$
 \varepsilon_1(0)=1,
 \qquad
 \varepsilon_1(q)=q\quad(q\ne0),
 \tag{2.2}
$$

and fix an arbitrary permutation

$$
 a\in\operatorname{Sym}(E).
 \tag{2.3}
$$

For each label $r\in Q$, the normalized partial return is

$$
 \phi_r=\varepsilon_1p^ra:E\longrightarrow E.
 \tag{2.4}
$$

Let $T_0$ be a five-element source-label set. A normalized state is an
injection $\lambda:T_0\hookrightarrow E$. The transition
$\lambda\xrightarrow{r}\phi_r\lambda$ is defined exactly when $\phi_r$ is
injective on the complete image $\lambda(T_0)$. For a fixed source
$\lambda_0$, write

$$
 \mathcal L_a(\lambda_0)
 \tag{2.5}
$$

for the complete set of injections reachable by finite guarded paths. No
length budget is imposed.

For an injection $\lambda$, let $\operatorname{ord}(\lambda)$ be the oriented
cyclic order obtained by reading the five source labels around the punctured
lane and deleting holes. Orders are taken modulo cyclic rotation, not
reflection.

We use two inherited one-lane results. Paper XXXII, Theorem 4.3 supplies
identity-branch order-fiber transitivity, while Paper XXXIV, Lemma 3.1
supplies arbitrary-branch identity-path simulation [@paper32; @paper34].

**Lemma 2.1 (order-fiber saturation).** If
$\lambda\in\mathcal L_a(\lambda_0)$ and
$\operatorname{ord}(\nu)=\operatorname{ord}(\lambda)$, then
$\nu\in\mathcal L_a(\lambda_0)$.

**Proof.** By Paper XXXII, Theorem 4.3, identity-branch hole slides are
strongly connected on every fixed labelled cyclic-order fiber. Paper XXXIV,
Lemma 3.1 simulates such an identity path in the arbitrary branch-$a$ system
from the same source to the same target injection. Hence one reachable
representative saturates its complete order fiber. $\square$

The label-zero return will also be essential:

$$
 \phi_0=a.
 \tag{2.6}
$$

It is globally enabled because $a$ is a permutation of $E$.

## The Finite Cyclic-Order Law

Let

$$
 \mathfrak C_5=\operatorname{CycOrd}(T_0),
 \qquad
 |\mathfrak C_5|=24.
 \tag{3.1}
$$

Define the branch order relation by

$$
 \boxed{
 \omega R_a\omega'
 \iff
 \exists\lambda:T_0\hookrightarrow E:
 \operatorname{ord}(\lambda)=\omega,
 \quad
 \operatorname{ord}(a\lambda)=\omega'.}
 \tag{3.2}
$$

The relation need not be a function: the image order may depend on the gaps
and phase of a representative of $\omega$.

Put

$$
 \omega_0=\operatorname{ord}(\lambda_0),
 \qquad
 \Omega_a^{\rm reach}
 =\{\operatorname{ord}(\lambda):
   \lambda\in\mathcal L_a(\lambda_0)\}.
 \tag{3.3}
$$

**Theorem 3.1 (exact order reachability).**

$$
 \boxed{
 \Omega_a^{\rm reach}
 =\operatorname{Reach}_{R_a}(\omega_0).}
 \tag{3.4}
$$

**Proof.** In a guarded return $\varepsilon_1p^ra$, the factor
$\varepsilon_1p^r$ preserves the hole-deleted cyclic label order. Thus every
actual return induces one $R_a$-edge. Conversely, suppose a reachable order
$\omega$ has an $R_a$-edge to $\omega'$. Choose an injection realizing that
edge. Lemma 2.1 makes this representative reachable. The globally enabled
label-zero return $a_\#$ then realizes the edge by an actual guarded step.
$\square$

### Eight relative patterns

Let $G=\operatorname{Sym}(T_0)\cong S_5$ act on cyclic orders by simultaneous
source-label relabelling. If $\lambda$ witnesses $\omega R_a\omega'$, then
$\lambda\tau^{-1}$ witnesses
$(\tau\omega)R_a(\tau\omega')$. Hence $R_a$ is diagonal $G$-invariant.

Take $\omega_0$ as base order and put

$$
 H=\operatorname{Stab}_G(\omega_0)\cong C_5.
 \tag{3.5}
$$

Then $\mathfrak C_5\cong G/H$, and the diagonal orbitals on
$\mathfrak C_5^2$ are indexed by $H\backslash G/H$. If
$\omega=g\omega_0$ and $\omega'=g'\omega_0$, define

$$
 [\omega,\omega']_{\rm rel}=Hg^{-1}g'H.
 \tag{3.6}
$$

The branch signature is

$$
 \boxed{
 \mathcal P_5(a)
 =\{[\omega,\omega']_{\rm rel}:\omega R_a\omega'\}
 \subseteq H\backslash G/H.}
 \tag{3.7}
$$

**Proposition 3.2 (exact pattern reconstruction).**

$$
 \boxed{
 \omega R_a\omega'
 \iff
 [\omega,\omega']_{\rm rel}\in\mathcal P_5(a).}
 \tag{3.8}
$$

**Proof.** Each member of $\mathcal P_5(a)$ names one diagonal $G$-orbit.
Diagonal invariance includes the complete orbital once any one of its edges is
present. $\square$

There are eight relative pattern classes. Burnside's lemma gives

$$
 |H\backslash G/H|
 =\frac{1}{5}(24+4\cdot4)
 =8.
 \tag{3.9}
$$

Thus $\mathcal P_5(a)$ is an exact eight-bit description of the edge law,
after any ordering of the eight classes. It does not replace the 24 vertices
on which reachability is computed.

### The lineage-order subgroup

For each double coset $D\in\mathcal P_5(a)$ choose one representative
$g_D\in G$ and define

$$
 \boxed{
 K_a^{\rm ord}
 =\langle H,g_D:D\in\mathcal P_5(a)\rangle
 \le S_5.}
 \tag{3.10}
$$

The subgroup is independent of the representatives because it already
contains $H$.

**Proposition 3.3 (order subgroup description).** With the fixed left-coset
action convention, the reachable-order set is the left-coset space

$$
 \boxed{
 \Omega_a^{\rm reach}\cong K_a^{\rm ord}/H.}
 \tag{3.11}
$$

**Proof.** Proposition 3.2 identifies $R_a$ with the union of the orbitals
indexed by $Hg_DH$. Starting from $H$, an edge appends an element of one such
double coset. The reachable cosets therefore lie in $K_a^{\rm ord}/H$.
Conversely each representative edge is present, while left and right factors
in $H$ only choose coset representatives. Repeated edges generate the finite
semigroup generated by the $g_D$ and $H$, which is the subgroup
$K_a^{\rm ord}$. $\square$

The superscript distinguishes $K_a^{\rm ord}$, which acts on five source
labels, from the physical return group on carrier coordinates in Paper XXX.

**Theorem A (order reduction).** The branch order law satisfies

$$
 \boxed{
 \omega R_a\omega'
 \iff
 [\omega,\omega']_{\rm rel}\in\mathcal P_5(a),
 \qquad
 \Omega_a^{\rm reach}
 =\operatorname{Reach}_{R_a}(\omega_0)
 \cong K_a^{\rm ord}/H.}
 \tag{3.12}
$$

The final term is the left-coset space of $H$ in $K_a^{\rm ord}$; no
quotient-group structure is asserted.

**Proof.** Combine Theorem 3.1, Proposition 3.2, and Proposition 3.3.
$\square$

## Terminal-Phase Collapse

Fix a candidate fused pair $F\subset T_0$, $|F|=2$, and put
$R_0=T_0\setminus F$. A formal pre-collapse terminal placement is an
injection

$$
 \mu:T_0\hookrightarrow Q,
 \qquad
 \mu(F)=\{0,1\}.
 \tag{4.1}
$$

The terminal exponent must select a hole of $\mu$:

$$
 0\notin p^{-u}\mu(T_0)
 \iff
 u\notin\mu(T_0).
 \tag{4.2}
$$

For a hole $u$, the candidate normalized source is

$$
 \lambda_u=a^{-1}p^{-u}\mu:T_0\hookrightarrow E.
 \tag{4.3}
$$

Define the phase fiber by

$$
 \Phi_a(\mu)
 =\{u\in Q\setminus\mu(T_0):
     \lambda_u\in\mathcal L_a(\lambda_0)\}.
 \tag{4.4}
$$

**Lemma 4.1 (branch invariance).**

$$
 \boxed{
 a_\#\mathcal L_a(\lambda_0)=\mathcal L_a(\lambda_0).}
 \tag{4.5}
$$

**Proof.** The globally enabled label-zero return gives the forward
inclusion. Since $a$ has finite order, a positive power of the same return
realizes $a^{-1}$ and gives the reverse inclusion. $\square$

Let $\operatorname{ord}_Q(\mu)$ be the oriented cyclic label order in the
full carrier $Q$.

**Theorem 4.2 (all-hole terminal-phase collapse; Theorem B).** For every
formal placement $\mu$,

$$
 \boxed{
 \Phi_a(\mu)=
 \begin{cases}
 Q\setminus\mu(T_0),
 &\operatorname{ord}_Q(\mu)\in\Omega_a^{\rm reach},\\[1mm]
 \varnothing,
 &\operatorname{ord}_Q(\mu)\notin\Omega_a^{\rm reach}.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{4.6}
$$

**Proof.** Fix a hole $u$. Lemma 4.1 gives

$$
 a^{-1}p^{-u}\mu\in\mathcal L_a
 \iff
 p^{-u}\mu\in\mathcal L_a.
 \tag{4.7}
$$

The rotation $p^{-u}$ moves an empty coordinate to $0$. Deleting that empty
coordinate leaves the full cyclic label order unchanged. By Lemma 2.1, the
right side of (4.7) holds exactly when
$\operatorname{ord}_Q(\mu)\in\Omega_a^{\rm reach}$. This criterion is
independent of the chosen hole. $\square$

**Corollary 4.3 (phase multiplicity).** Every attainable placement has
exactly $n-5$ terminal phase witnesses. An unattainable placement has none.

**Corollary 4.4 (same-witness terminal criterion).**

$$
 \boxed{
 \mu\text{ is attainable}
 \iff
 \operatorname{ord}_Q(\mu)\in\Omega_a^{\rm reach}.}
 \tag{4.8}
$$

For every selected hole $u$, the injection
$\lambda=a^{-1}p^{-u}\mu$ is reachable and satisfies
$p^ua\lambda=\mu$. Thus no endpoint is promoted without an actual source and
the same terminal exponent.

**Corollary 4.5 (order-law phase descent).** For the same source cyclic order,

$$
 \mathcal P_5(a)=\mathcal P_5(b)
 \Longrightarrow
 \Omega_a^{\rm reach}=\Omega_b^{\rm reach}
 \Longrightarrow
 \Phi_a=\Phi_b.
 \tag{4.9}
$$

## Complete Survivor and Frontier Classification

Choose a temporary naming $F=\{f_0,f_1\}$ and let
$R_0=\{r_1,r_2,r_3\}$. A linear order $(s_1,s_2,s_3)$ of $R_0$ is compatible
with $F$ if at least one of

$$
 [f_0,f_1,s_1,s_2,s_3],
 \qquad
 [f_1,f_0,s_1,s_2,s_3]
 \tag{5.1}
$$

belongs to $\Omega_a^{\rm reach}$. Let $M_a(F)$ be the set of compatible
orders and put

$$
 m_a(F)=|M_a(F)|,
 \qquad
 0\le m_a(F)\le6.
 \tag{5.2}
$$

The definition is independent of the temporary naming of the two elements of
$F$.

Let $\mathfrak S_a(\lambda_0;F)$ be the set of source-addressed survivor
injections obtained from actual terminal witnesses over $F$.

**Theorem C (complete one-lane survivor frontier).**

$$
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak S_a(\lambda_0;F)
 \cong
 M_a(F)\times\binom{Q\setminus\{0,1\}}{3}.}
 \tag{5.3}
$$

Consequently,

$$
 \boxed{
 |\mathfrak S_a(\lambda_0;F)|
 =m_a(F)\binom{n-2}{3}.}
 \tag{5.4}
$$

**Proof.** Let $\{c_1<c_2<c_3\}$ be a three-element subset of
$Q\setminus\{0,1\}$ and $(s_1,s_2,s_3)\in M_a(F)$. Map $s_i$ to $c_i$ and
map the two labels in $F$ to $0,1$ in an orientation witnessing membership
in (5.1). This defines a formal terminal placement whose cyclic order belongs
to $\Omega_a^{\rm reach}$. Corollary 4.4 supplies an actual same-witness
terminal realization.

Conversely, every terminal witness has a unique survivor coordinate set and
a unique linear order of the three survivor labels on that set. Its full
cyclic order is reachable, so the survivor order lies in $M_a(F)$. The two
constructions are inverse. $\square$

**Corollary 5.1 (fused-pair criterion).** The pair $F$ is hittable if and
only if $m_a(F)>0$.

**Corollary 5.2 (complete normalized raw frontier).** The complete strict-exit
incidence over $F$ is obtained from (5.3) by mapping both fused labels to the
collapse image $1$ and retaining the survivor injection. Hence the relative
order law determines the complete one-lane raw frontier.

**Corollary 5.3 (complete terminal-incidence invariant).** For branches with
the same source cyclic order,

$$
 \boxed{
 \mathcal P_5(a)=\mathcal P_5(b)
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathfrak S_a(\lambda_0;F)
 =\mathfrak S_b(\lambda_0;F)
 \quad\text{for every }F.}
 \tag{5.5}
$$

**Proof.** Equal signatures give the same order subgroup and reachable-order
spectrum by Theorem A. They therefore give the same $M_a(F)$ for every fused
pair. Theorem C then gives identical survivor spectra. $\square$

Complete here means that the signature determines the terminal-incidence
frontier. Minimality is not asserted, and the converse implication is not
claimed.

### Dihedral recovery

For an orientation-preserving dihedral branch, $m_a(F)=1$ on the five pairs
that are consecutive in the source cyclic order and vanishes otherwise. For
an orientation-reversing branch, the corresponding value is $2$. Thus
Theorem C recovers the Paper XXXIV spectra

$$
 \binom{n-2}{3}
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 2\binom{n-2}{3}.
 \tag{5.6}
$$

## Controls, Scope, and Open Boundary

### Six-point exact control

The paper-owned finite package exhausts all $5!=120$ branch permutations at
$n=6$. It independently computes the 24-state relation, its eight double-
coset patterns, guarded injection reachability, terminal witnesses, phase
fibers, survivor spectra, and boundary incidence. All branches satisfy the
phase-collapse and survivor-count formulas.

At $n=6$, every injection is a bijection. The eight pattern classes have sizes

$$
 5,5,5,5,25,25,25,25
 \tag{6.1}
$$

and collapse to the five overgroups

$$
 C_5,\quad D_{10},\quad
 F_{20}\cong C_5\rtimes C_4\cong\operatorname{AGL}(1,5),\quad
 A_5,\quad S_5.
 \tag{6.2}
$$

This no-internal-hole subgroup classification is a finite structural control.
The all-$n$ theorems above are proved independently of it.

### What remains open

The result is deliberately restricted in four ways.

1. It is one-lane. The multi-lane capacity and same-witness mobility
   obstruction of Paper XXXIII remains.
2. It is unbudgeted. No shortest witness or word-length theorem is asserted.
3. It classifies the frontier from $\mathcal P_5(a)$ but does not give a
   simpler carrier-level formula for which branch permutations realize each
   signature.
4. It is raw. No typed transfer, authorization, ancestry, projectability, or
   recursive-return statement follows.

The remaining bridge to typed semantics requires two further steps:

$$
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{raw complete survivor frontier}\\
 \Downarrow\\
 \text{existing-semantics raw-to-typed bridge}\\
 \Downarrow\\
 \text{same-witness projectability}
 \end{gathered}}
 \tag{6.3}
$$

Both arrows remain open under the existing typed semantics.

## Conclusion

One-lane terminal incidence is controlled by cyclic-order reachability. The
branch law on 24 cyclic orders compresses to eight relative pattern classes,
and those classes generate an order subgroup of $S_5$. Global branch
invertibility then removes terminal phase as an independent acceptance
condition: every hole of a reachable placement is valid. The remaining raw
frontier is exactly the product of a finite set of survivor orders and a
three-coordinate subset.

Thus, within the declared one-lane system,

$$
 \boxed{
 \text{relative order law}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{all terminal phases}
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{complete source-addressed survivor frontier}.}
$$

The unresolved problems now lie outside that collapse: multi-lane mobility,
length-sensitive witnesses, and the existing-semantics bridge from raw
incidence to typed transfer and projectability.

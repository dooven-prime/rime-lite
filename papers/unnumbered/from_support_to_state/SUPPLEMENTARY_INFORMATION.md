# Supplementary Information

## A Path Is Not a State: Exact Predictive Refinement of Fixed Connectome Partitions

WuJun Chen

This document retains audit-domain, diagnostic, and finite-data details
supporting the journal manuscript. It does not enlarge the theorem domain
or replace the published evidence identities of RIME Papers XVI and XVII.

## S1. Distinct Audit Domains

The static coverage audit uses a nonnegative full-release edge table. The
same-carrier liftability and first-order comparison use the signed,
normalized annotation-intersection carrier. The reached-set follow-up uses
711 selected unit sources and exact rational orbits through horizon four.
These are different finite domains even when they refer to the same
underlying connectome release.

**Table S1. Audited domains, observations, and evidence used in the manuscript.**

| Audit | Domain and observation | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Static coverage | Three nested sector-universe policies on nonnegative adjacency | Exact finite route counts |
| Same-carrier liftability | Signed normalized carrier; somaSide and superclass; depths 2 and 3 | Exact finite product decisions |
| First-order scan | Eligible unit-state domain; superclass or somaSide means | Published numerical successor differences |
| Exact first-order obstruction | Two previously recorded somaSide unit sources | Two-row rational certificate |
| Profile-heterogeneity census | All 163,439 eligible unit sources; both registered mean observations | Exact class, source-pair, and coordinate-separation counts |
| Named one-lag obstruction | Declared history-fiber search; superclass means | Exact named-sector witness |
| Reached-set follow-up | 711 selected sources; superclass means; horizon 4 | Exact rational retained-orbit audits |

The signed liftability result is an exact finite computational certificate.
The previously published first-order scan is numerical; the added two-row
certificate proves one exact inequality for the rational model, not the
exactness of every scan entry. The profile census groups rational profiles
through their canonical integer keys and uses bounded exact integer
accumulation. It covers eligible unit
sources, not arbitrary later activity vectors. The reached-set follow-up
starts from retained exact-orbit artifacts and does not regenerate
microscopic trajectories from the upstream connectome.

## S2. Diagnostic Sequence

The procedure fixes a carrier, its action convention, an update, an
observation, source states, and a finite recorded horizon. Static route
tests are separate from dynamic factorization tests.

**Table S2. Finite diagnostic sequence for comparing observation histories.**

| Step | Check and retained output | Interpretation controlled |
|---|---|---|
| Fix the domain | Choose one common set of source-time addresses for every history order | A changing left time boundary is not a memory effect |
| Compare history partitions | Compare fiber memberships and successor constancy, not only value counts | Equal partitions mean added lags do not distinguish more points on that domain |
| Inspect residual noninjectivity | Separate singleton fibers from distinct states sharing an observation; inspect the update | A passing fiber test may be sustained by common leakage after drive clipping |
| Check iteration | Test successor membership in the verified image and factor identity on successive microscopic domains | A one-step factor need not define an image endomap |

For a negative first-order result, retain a source pair and an exact
successor-coordinate difference where the arithmetic permits it. For a
positive result, retain the tested domain and complete fiber membership.
Neither is a certificate for untested sources or times.

## S3. Coverage Increments

The nonnegative depth-two audit uses three nested sector-universe policies.
Here `<missing>` denotes a body whose curated annotation value is absent;
`<unannotated>` denotes a body absent from the curated annotation table.
Typed core excludes the latter label at all three positions. Relay strict
allows it at endpoints, and coverage inclusive also allows it at the
middle body. These policy increments therefore admit different candidate
populations.

**Table S3. Incremental supported and lifted triples under policy enlargement.**

| Enlargement | Support gain | Lift gain |
|---|---:|---:|
| Typed core to relay strict | 782 | 781 |
| Relay strict to coverage inclusive | 812 | 374 |

The first increment has one nonlifting candidate. In the second, 438 of
the 812 new triples have no common microscopic relay. The lifted count
increases in both increments, although the overall liftability fraction
falls in the second. For old supported and lifted counts $B,L$ and a new
increment $\Delta B>0,\Delta L$, the ratio difference is the elementary
identity

\[
\frac{L+\Delta L}{B+\Delta B}-\frac LB
=\frac{\Delta B}{B+\Delta B}
 \left(\frac{\Delta L}{\Delta B}-\frac LB\right).
\]

The audit checks incoming/outgoing relay intersections body by body.
It does not infer a causal effect of missing annotation.

## S4. Historical First-Reference Scan

Within each observation sector, the registered first-reference scan
selects the first eligible node in the source order and compares later
nodes with that reference. Its witness count is not an all-pairs count
or a prevalence estimate; the defect is one witnessed discrepancy, not
the diameter of a fiber.

**Table S4. Published first-reference one-step observation defects.**

| Partition | Sectors | Witnesses | Largest L1 defect |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| superclass | 28 | 141,296 | 0.005651574473947287 |
| somaSide | 4 | 163,435 | 0.0004874856094383272 |

The defect magnitudes do not rank the partitions, which differ in
dimension, sector sizes, and compression.

## S5. Coordinate-Resolved Pair Counts

For source sector $E_i$ and target coordinate $j$, let $n_{ijq}$ count
sources with profile coordinate $q$. The exact number of unordered
same-observation source pairs separated by $j$ is

\[
D_{ij}=\binom{N_i}{2}-\sum_q\binom{n_{ijq}}{2}.
\]

**Table S5. Absolute somaSide pair separations by target coordinate.**

| Target coordinate | Separated within-sector source pairs |
|---|---:|
| L | 5,008,763,423 |
| R | 4,993,360,210 |
| `<missing>` | 3,413,153,081 |
| M | 1,307,422,058 |

The common denominator is 5,432,178,301 unordered within-sector pairs.
Pairs separated by several coordinates appear in several rows, so the
counts are not additive. The manuscript's Figure 2 visualizes normalized
percentages; these exact counts are its source-resolved counterpart.

## S6. Exact Histories on the Common Reached Set

The Paper XVII follow-up selected 711 of 163,439 eligible sources across
25 nonempty source strata. One source per stratum was retained before a
capacity-based largest-remainder allocation; SHA-256 ranking then selected
members within strata. The selection rule did not inspect trajectory
outcomes. Exact rational orbits were retained through horizon four.

The original history-order comparison used different left boundaries:
current observations at $t=0,\ldots,3$, one lag at $t=1,\ldots,3$, and
two lags at $t=2,3$. The common comparison domain is
$D_0\times\{2,3\}$.

**Table S6. History-fiber counts on the common 1,422-window domain.**

| History order | Windows | Distinct history values | Largest fiber | Failure fibers |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 1,422 | 1,228 | 51 | 0 |
| 1 | 1,422 | 1,228 | 51 | 0 |
| 2 | 1,422 | 1,228 | 51 | 0 |

The three partitions, not merely their summary counts, are equal on this
domain. Six unsafe fibers at $t=1$ contain 51 sources; all have singleton
observations at $t=2$ in the selected source set. Five separate persistent
cohorts of sizes 51, 25, 19, 4, and 3 remain noninjective with the same
membership at $t=2$ and $t=3$. These slices account for
$2[(51-1)+(25-1)+(19-1)+(4-1)+(3-1)]=194$ redundant source-time windows.
The manuscript's Appendix A gives the distinct-state and drive-off
interpretation of these retained cohorts.

For the earlier superclass one-lag audit, equality was checked using the
canonical integer key encoding the rational profile

\[
\left(i(a),s_a/g_a,p_{a1}/g_a,\ldots,p_{am}/g_a\right),\qquad
g_a=\gcd(s_a,p_{a1},\ldots,p_{am}).
\]

A named-sector follow-up found an exact witness in `cb_intrinsic`: the
one-lag histories agree while nine next-observation coordinates differ.
This is a separate domain from the 711-source positive follow-up.

## S7. Reproduction and Provenance

The published source packages remain independently identified as
[Paper XVI](https://github.com/dooven-prime/rime-lite/tree/paper16-v1.0/experiments/paper16)
and [Paper XVII](https://github.com/dooven-prime/rime-lite/blob/paper17-v1.0/experiments/paper17/00_START_HERE.md).
Paper XVI binds upstream MaleCNS data, selected carrier files, static
audits, and dynamic records. Paper XVII starts its public checks from
retained exact-orbit artifacts and transition-cache records.

The predictive-profile Dataset (DOI:
[10.5281/zenodo.23555888](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23555888))
contains the exact full-domain census, its source and seven focused controls,
the pinned derived carrier,
and both figure sources. Its default verifier checks the full file inventory
and byte digests; optional source replay recomputes the census. The separate
two-source witness supplement contains the exact certificate, its checker,
six controls, and the three Paper XVI metadata records it reads. Its default
check verifies compact arithmetic without the large carrier. To re-extract
the two source rows, pass the carrier directory from the extracted Dataset
to the witness verifier's `--replay --carrier-dir` option. A compact check
alone does not establish that the rows were extracted from the carrier.
Neither replay is independent replication or reconstruction of the original
upstream connectome pipeline.

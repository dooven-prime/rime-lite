## Abstract

**Problem.** Fixed-depth finite-field route profiles do not by themselves
classify the characteristic-zero state space obtained by holding defect fixed.

**Approach.** Rational defect dynamics is defined on primitive cusps of
$\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$, with states represented by oriented anchored patches
of the Farey tessellation.

**Results.** Every fixed-deficit orbit slice is finite. The reachable layer
$\mathcal C_k$ is exactly the set of anchored Farey boundaries of cardinality
$k$, with
$|\mathcal C_k|=\operatorname{Cat}_k+\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}$ for
$k\geq1$. Relative continuant bounds give exact coordinate, internal, global,
and guarded Fibonacci envelopes. Reduction is a labelled graph isomorphism
through deficit $k$ over every finite field of characteristic
$p>F_{2k+1}$.

**Boundary.** Exact integer certificates through deficit ten, including an
independent boundary generator and a hostile $p=23$ registry collision,
support but do not replace the all-deficit proofs. The sufficient stabilization
threshold is not claimed to be sharp.

## Introduction

### Problem and scope

Paper XXI studies finite-field route semantics at fixed routing depth
\cite{chen2026paper21}. Its low-deficit survivor layers motivate a distinct
limit: this paper fixes deficit $k$ while characteristic tends to infinity,
rather than fixing depth $d$ while field size grows. The resulting questions
are whether the characteristic-zero state space is finite, whether it has an
intrinsic combinatorial classification, and whether arithmetic data control
finite-field stabilization. The basic objects are finite sets of primitive
cusps, and the governing geometry is the Farey tessellation.

### Main results

The main results are:

| result | statement |
|---|---|
| Theorem 1 | every cumulative rational low-deficit state space is finite and effectively enumerable |
| Theorem 2 | reachable states are exactly anchored Farey boundaries |
| Corollary 3 | $|\mathcal C_k|=\operatorname{Cat}_k+\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}$ for $k\geq1$ |
| Theorem 4 | the height, internal, global, and guard determinant extrema are $F_{k+1},F_k,F_{2k},F_{2k+1}$ |
| Theorem 5 | characteristic $p>F_{2k+1}$ gives a labelled graph isomorphism through deficit $k$ |

The classification uses finite oriented dual patches of the Farey tessellation,
explicit re-rooting, leaf attachment, and predecessor induction. Catalan
counting comes from the forced cusp $1$ on every nontrivial positive path. The
Fibonacci result is unified by cancellation of common root words and a finite
typed bridge calculation in the rooted Farey flag graph.

## Related Work and Novelty Boundary

The Farey tessellation, its $PSL_2(\mathbb Z)$ action, and the coding of modular orbits
by continued fractions are classical; Series gives a geometric formulation in
which the rational cusps are precisely the ideal vertices of the Farey
tessellation \cite{series1985}. Binary trees of positive rationals, mediant insertion, and
their matrix encodings are also standard Stern-Brocot/Calkin-Wilf material
\cite{graham1994,calkinWilf2000}. Continuants and their extremal arrangements
form an established subject \cite{graham1994,ramharter1983}, and the general
Catalan convolution used in Section 4 is standard \cite{stanley2015}.

The present claims do not concern a new construction of the Farey tessellation,
a new enumeration of all rationals, or a new general extremal theorem for
continuants. The new surface is the typed defect system generated by the three
declared maps $\Phi_S$, $\Phi_R$, and $\Phi_{R^{-1}}$: the exact classification of its
reachable fixed-deficit states by three anchored boundary types, the separation
of configuration-internal, global, and one-step guard determinant universes,
and the resulting labelled finite-field graph stabilization theorem. The
finite bridge calculation in Section 5 specializes standard continuant
machinery to those typed registries; it is included in full because the bridge
corrections are part of the theorem, not a convention inherited from the
general literature.

## Rational Defect Dynamics

### Notation and exact candidate class

Write

$$
\mathcal{C}_k=\{\text{reachable rational defect states of cardinality }k\},
\qquad
\operatorname{Cat}_k=\frac{1}{k+1}\binom{2k}{k}.
$$

The deficit-zero state is exceptional and is fixed separately:

$$
\mathcal{C}_0=\{\varnothing\}.
$$

Write

$$
\mathcal{C}_{\leq k}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{k}\mathcal{C}_j
$$

for the cumulative rational state space. The finite-field analogue is

$$
\mathcal{C}_{\leq k}^{(F)}=\bigcup_{j=0}^k\mathcal{C}_j^{(F)}.
$$

For all rational cusp calculations, fix the canonical primitive vectors

$$
v_\infty=(1,0),\qquad
v_{n/d}=(n,d),\qquad
d>0,\quad \gcd(|n|,d)=1.
$$

This single convention governs Farey adjacency, reduction, and every
determinant universe below.

### Definition 2.1 (Farey boundary)

Let $B$ be a finite subset of $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$. If its
projective-line cyclic order is

$$
(x_0,\ldots,x_m),
$$

call $B$ a **Farey boundary** when, for the canonical primitive vectors
$v_i=v_{x_i}$,

$$
|\det(v_i,v_{i+1})|=1
\qquad(i\bmod m+1).
$$

For two vertices, the same Farey edge is traversed in both cyclic directions;
this is the declared degenerate boundary convention. A singleton is not a
Farey boundary.

For a nonempty defect state $D$, put

$$
\widehat D=D\cup\{\infty\}.
$$

Call $D$ an **anchored Farey state** when $\widehat D$ is a Farey boundary and

$$
D\cap\{0,-1\}\ne\varnothing.
$$

The hatted cyclic condition is essential. The weak conditions "contains an
anchor" and "successive finite cusps have determinant one" admit the infinite
false family $\{0,1/n\}$. Its closing edge from $1/n$ to infinity has
determinant $n$ and fails Definition 2.1 unless $n=1$.

There are three disjoint marked types.

1. **zero-only:** the boundary consists of the base edge $\{0,\infty\}$ and a
   positive Farey path on its other side.
2. **negative-one-only:** the analogous boundary based at
   $\{\infty,-1\}$.
3. **both-anchors:** the boundary encloses the base ideal triangle
   $\{\infty,-1,0\}$ and may extend across each of its three sides.

### Lemma 2.2 (anchor-type decomposition)

Every anchored Farey state belongs to exactly one of the three marked types
above.  In particular, the anchor condition is not merely a requirement that
one of $0,-1$ occurs: it forces the corresponding base edge or base triangle.

**Proof.** Consider first the case
$$
D\cap\{0,-1\}=\{0\}.
$$

The boundary has two arcs from $\infty$ to $0$. Under $x\mapsto-x$, every
nontrivial arc on the negative side becomes a nontrivial positive Farey path.
Every such positive path contains $1$: if Farey neighbors $a/b<1<c/d$ skipped
$1$, then $bc-ad=b(c-d)+d(b-a)\geq b+d\geq2$, contrary to determinant one.
Hence every nontrivial negative-side arc contains $-1$. Since $-1$ is absent,
that arc is the base edge $\{\infty,0\}$, and the complementary arc is the
positive path in the definition of zero-only.

The integral symmetry $x\mapsto-1-x$ fixes $\infty$ and exchanges $0$ with
$-1$. It gives the negative-one-only case and forces the base edge
$\{\infty,-1\}$ whenever $0$ is absent.

If both anchors occur, their cyclic order is $(\infty,-1,0)$ up to reversal.
The simple boundary splits into three anchor-to-anchor Farey arcs. Each arc
lies on the exterior side of the corresponding edge of the base triangle;
otherwise two boundary arcs would cross. Thus the base ideal triangle
$\{\infty,-1,0\}$ lies inside the patch, and each exterior arc normalizes to a
positive Farey path. The three cases are disjoint by their anchor intersections
and exhaustive. `QED`

### Deficit transition and finite-field model

Fix the alphabet $\mathcal A=\{S,R,R^{-1}\}$ and the declared integral
representatives

$$
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\widetilde{R^{-1}}
=-R_{\mathrm{alg}}^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
$$

where the algebraic matrix inverse is

$$
R_{\mathrm{alg}}^{-1}
=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

For $a\in\mathcal A$, let $\widetilde a$ denote its declared representative;
write $a(x)$ for the induced projective action. Thus the alphabet label
$R^{-1}$ acts through $\widetilde{R^{-1}}$. Replacing a representative by its
negative does not change the cusp map, but the displayed lifts are fixed for
all determinant and bridge calculations.

For $a\in\mathcal A$, define

$$
\Phi_a(D)=a(D\cup\{\infty\})\setminus\{\infty\}.
$$

If $\pi_a=a^{-1}(\infty)$ is the pole of $a$, then the modular action is
injective on cusps and therefore

$$
|\Phi_a(D)|=|D|+1-\mathbf 1_{\{\pi_a\in D\}}.
$$

This is the basic deficit monotonicity law: a transition either attaches one
new cusp or re-roots at the same deficit. The primitive cusp model makes every
fixed rational layer exact and discrete.

For a finite field $F=F_q$ of characteristic $p$, write
$\mathbb P^1(F)=F\cup\{\infty\}$ and let $\bar a$ be the reduction of the
declared representative $\widetilde a$ over $F$. Define the finite-field
transition intrinsically by

$$
\Phi_{a,F}(D)=\bar a(D\cup\{\infty\})\setminus\{\infty\},
\qquad
\mathcal{C}_k^{(F)}
 =\{D:\ |D|=k,\ D\text{ is reachable from }\varnothing\text{ by the }\Phi_{a,F}\}.
$$

For $F=F_p$, set $\Phi_{a,p}=\Phi_{a,F_p}$ and
$\mathcal{C}_k^{(p)}=\mathcal{C}_k^{(F_p)}$. Because the declared matrices have
entries in the prime field and the initial cusp is $\infty$, every reachable
finite-field cusp lies in
$\mathbb P^1(F_p)\subseteq\mathbb P^1(F_q)$. Thus the finite-field graph is
defined uniformly for every finite field.

### Lemma 2.3 (anchor and same-level transport)

Every nonempty successor contains $0$ or $-1$. If a transition preserves the
deficit, then

$$
\widehat{\Phi_a(D)}=a\widehat D.
$$

**Proof.** The image of infinity is $0$ for $S,R$ and $-1$ for $R^{-1}$, so it
is finite in every successor. If the deficit is preserved, the pole belongs
to $D$; hence infinity is in $a(D)$ and the hatted identity follows after
removing and restoring it. `QED`

### Lemma 2.4 (two-cusp rigidity)

An element of $PSL_2(\mathbb Z)$ fixing two distinct rational cusps pointwise
is the identity. Consequently, for fixed distinct rational cusps $x,y$ and
targets $x',y'$, at most one modular element sends $(x,y)$ to $(x',y')$.

**Proof.** Two fixed rational eigenlines force the two eigenvalues of a
determinant-one integral matrix to be rational algebraic integers with product
one. They are therefore both $1$ or both $-1$, giving $I$ or $-I$, the same
element in $PSL_2(\mathbb Z)$. Compose two proposed transports for the uniqueness
claim. `QED`

### Lemma 2.5 (finite anchored orbit slice)

For a finite cusp configuration $X$, let

$$
\mathcal N(X)=\{gX:\infty\in gX,
\quad gX\cap\{0,-1\}\ne\varnothing,\quad g\in PSL_2(\mathbb Z)\}.
$$

Then $\mathcal N(X)$ is finite and

$$
|\mathcal N(X)|\leq 2|X|(|X|-1).
$$

**Proof.** For a normalized image choose a preimage of infinity, a distinct
preimage of one declared anchor, and the anchor itself. There are at most
$2|X|(|X|-1)$ choices. Lemma 2.4 gives at most one group element per choice and
hence at most one image. `QED`

**Theorem 1 (fixed-deficit finiteness).** For every $k$, the cumulative
reachable set $\mathcal{C}_{\leq k}$ is finite and effectively enumerable.

**Proof.** Induct on $k$. From finitely many states below level $k$, there are
finitely many first transitions that grow to level $k$. After the first such
transition, every remaining transition preserves size. Lemma 2.3 identifies the
hatted endpoint with a modular image of the finite seed, and the anchor is
present, so all such endpoints lie in the finite orbit slice of Lemma 2.5. A
finite union over the seeds proves finiteness, and exact breadth-first closure
terminates. `QED`

The finite-field comparison is postponed until the guarded registry has been
bounded arithmetically in Section 5. Theorem 5 is stated and proved there as
an explicit reduction-map graph isomorphism, rather than as an appeal to the
finite-field precursor.

## Anchored Farey Classification

### Theorem 2 (anchored Farey classification)

For every $k\geq1$,

$$
D\in\mathcal{C}_k
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
D\text{ is an anchored Farey state of cardinality }k.
$$

The proof is organized on the Farey tessellation rather than directly on
rational-coordinate formulas.

### Lemma 3.1 (boundary/patch correspondence)

Every nondegenerate Farey boundary uniquely bounds a finite connected union of
Farey ideal triangles. Its dual is a finite connected subtree of the trivalent
dual Farey tree. A two-vertex boundary is treated as the empty patch rooted at
its declared Farey edge.

**Proof.** Suppose the boundary has $n\geq3$ ideal vertices. Its cyclically
ordered Farey edges form a simple ideal $n$-gon. The fixed Farey tessellation,
rather than an abstract choice of polygon triangulation, determines the set of
Farey triangles contained in this polygon. The polygon has hyperbolic area
$(n-2)\pi$, while every Farey ideal triangle has area $\pi$; their interiors are
disjoint. Hence the patch contains exactly $n-2$ triangles and is finite.

The polygon interior is connected, so the adjacency dual of these triangles
is connected. It has $n-2$ vertices and $n-3$ edges, one for each internal
diagonal of the induced triangulation. Therefore its edge count is one less
than its vertex count, and the connected dual is a tree. The contained set of
Farey triangles is uniquely determined by the fixed tessellation. The
two-vertex convention is the separately declared empty-patch case. `QED`

Under this correspondence:

- a zero-only state is a finite rooted patch attached on the non-base side
  of $\{0,\infty\}$;
- a negative-one-only state is attached on the non-base side of
  $\{\infty,-1\}$;
- a both-anchors state contains the base triangle and consists of three
  rooted side patches attached to it.

This is the patch object. Its orientation and root flag are retained data;
unoriented abstract-tree isomorphism alone is too coarse.

### Lemma 3.2 (transition conjugacy)

For $a\in\{S,R,R^{-1}\}$, let $\pi_a=a^{-1}(\infty)$. Exact set algebra gives

$$
\widehat{\Phi_a(D)}=
\begin{cases}
a\widehat D,&\pi_a\in D,\\
a\widehat D\cup\{\infty\},&\pi_a\notin D.
\end{cases}
$$

In the first case the modular isometry preserves the oriented dual patch and
changes the flag identified with the standard rooted flag. This is a
same-deficit **re-root** move.

In the second case the state has exactly one anchor. Its declared base boundary
edge is carried to an edge of the new standard Farey triangle:

| label | missing pole | carried boundary edge | attached triangle |
|---|---|---|---|
| $R$ | $-1$ | $\{\infty,0\}\to\{0,-1\}$ | $\{\infty,0,-1\}$ |
| $S$ | $0$ | $\{\infty,-1\}\to\{0,1\}$ | $\{\infty,0,1\}$ |
| $R^{-1}$ | $0$ | $\{\infty,-1\}\to\{-1,0\}$ | $\{\infty,-1,0\}$ |

Adding infinity therefore attaches one exterior Farey triangle along the
carried root edge. In the dual tree this is an **attach-leaf** move. This table
also shows why the poles and labels are typed data; an arbitrary modular map
cannot be substituted without re-proving the root-edge statement.

### Lemma 3.3 (oriented root-flag connectivity)

Fix one finite oriented dual patch $P$. An oriented triangle flag consists of a
Farey triangle and one of its cyclically marked ideal vertices. Declare the
following flags admissible:

1. all three marked flags on every triangle of $P$;
2. for every boundary edge of $P$, the two flags on the exterior adjacent
   triangle whose marked vertex is an endpoint of that boundary edge.

Let $f_*$ be the standard positively oriented flag on
$(\infty,0,-1)$ with marked vertex $\infty$. The stabilizer of a positively
oriented Farey triangle is its cyclic order-three rotation group. Marking one
ideal vertex removes this residual stabilizer, so $PSL_2(\mathbb Z)$ acts simply
transitively on oriented triangle flags. For every flag $f$, let $g_f$ be the
unique element satisfying

$$
g_f(f)=f_*.
$$

The induced labelled action is fixed by the convention

$$
\sigma_a(f):=g_f^{-1}a^{-1}f_*.
$$

By simple transitivity this is the unique flag with

$$
g_{\sigma_a(f)}=a g_f,
\qquad
\widehat D_{\sigma_a(f)}=a\widehat D_f.
$$

Under this convention the intuitive clockwise/counterclockwise names of
$R$ and $R^{-1}$ may be interchanged; both labels are present, and only their
connectivity is used below.

For an admissible flag define the normalized defect state

$$
D_f=g_f(\partial P)\setminus\{\infty\},
$$

where $\partial P$ denotes the cusp set of the patch boundary. If $f$ lies on
an internal triangle, its other two triangle vertices show that $D_f$
contains both $0$ and $-1$. If $f$ is one of the declared exterior flags, the
shared boundary edge shows that $D_f$ contains exactly the anchor represented
by its other endpoint. Conversely, a flag normalization whose boundary
contains infinity and one of the two anchors is of one of these two forms.
Thus

$$
f\text{ is admissible}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
D_f\cap\{0,-1\}\ne\varnothing,
$$

and $D_f\cup\{\infty\}$ is the Farey boundary of the normalized patch.
The graph of admissible flags under same-deficit $S$, $R$, and $R^{-1}$ moves is
connected.

**Proof.** With the convention above, $f'=\sigma_a(f)$ is the unique flag
with $g_{f'}=a g_f$. The labels $R$ and $R^{-1}$ induce the two cyclic corner
rotations of one triangle, while $S$ crosses the edge incident to the
corresponding oriented corner. If
$\pi_a=a^{-1}(\infty)$ belongs to $D_f$, Lemma 3.2 gives

$$
\widehat{\Phi_a(D_f)}=a g_f(\partial P)
=g_{f'}(\partial P)=\widehat D_{f'}.
$$

Thus the labelled transition is same-deficit exactly when $f'$ remains in the
admissible flag set; equivalently, exactly when the pole-membership condition
holds. If $f'$ leaves that set, Lemma 3.2 identifies the move as attach-leaf
rather than a re-rooting of $P$.

All flags on one internal triangle are connected by the two rotations. Flags
on triangles sharing an internal edge are connected by a suitable rotation,
one $S$ crossing, and another rotation. Since the internal dual subtree is
connected, all internal flags are connected. Each declared exterior flag is
connected across its shared boundary edge to a flag on the incident internal
triangle. Hence the full admissible flag graph is connected. For the
two-vertex base-edge patch, the two edge orientations are connected directly
by $S$. `QED`

The exact transition census warns against a coarser statement. At $k=5$ the
whole same-level graph has four components of sizes $8,12,12,24$; at $k=6$ it
has six components of size $29$. Connectivity is asserted only inside a fixed
oriented patch class, not across all states of one deficit and not after
forgetting the orientation/flag data.

### Lemma 3.4 (admissible predecessor existence)

Let $P$ be a nonempty anchored patch. There exist an anchored patch $P^-$ with
one fewer triangle, an admissible state $D^-$ representing $P^-$, a label
$a\in\{S,R,R^{-1}\}$, and an admissible rooting $D'$ of $P$ such that

$$
\Phi_a(D^-)=D'.
$$

**Proof.** If the dual tree of $P$ has one vertex, then an admissible
normalization sends $P$ to the base ideal triangle. Choose either declared base
edge, say $\{\infty,0\}$, as the empty predecessor patch $P^-$. The
corresponding one-anchor state $D^-$ is
reachable at deficit one, and the appropriate attach-leaf row of Lemma 3.2
adds the third vertex and produces an admissible internal rooting $D'$ of
$P$. This is the $k=2$ base case.

Suppose now that the dual tree has at least two vertices. Choose a leaf
triangle $\tau$ and delete it, retaining its shared parent edge $e$ as a
boundary edge of the remaining oriented patch $P^-$. The result is connected
and is either a smaller finite patch or the declared edge-rooted empty patch;
the admissible normalization below supplies its anchor marking.

View $\tau$ as the exterior triangle adjacent to $e$ in $P^-$. Choose one of
the two exterior flags on $\tau$ whose marked cusp is an endpoint of $e$.
This is an admissible flag of $P^-$, so its standard normalization gives an
anchored one-anchor state $D^-$. The three cyclic placements of the oriented
edge $e$ are exactly the three attach-leaf rows of Lemma 3.2. Hence one of the
labels $a$ sends $D^-$ forward to an admissible internal rooting $D'$ of the
larger patch $P$, proving the displayed equality. This is a predecessor
construction: geometric deletion is not a deficit-decreasing transition.
`QED`

**Proof of Theorem 2.** The forward implication follows by induction from the
two edge-rooted deficit-one states. Lemma 3.2 shows that a same-level move only
re-roots the current patch and a growth move attaches one leaf, so every
reachable positive-deficit state is anchored Farey.

For the reverse implication, take an anchored Farey state $D$. Lemma 3.1 gives
its finite oriented dual patch $P$. If the patch is empty, $D$ is one of the
two deficit-one base states. Otherwise Lemma 3.4 supplies a smaller anchored
predecessor $D^-$, a label $a$, and a rooting $D'$ of $P$ with
$\Phi_a(D^-)=D'$. By induction $D^-$ is reachable, hence the forward growth
transition makes $D'$ reachable. Lemma 3.3 connects $D'$ to $D$ by same-level
moves. These moves are reversible: $S$ is an involution, $R$ and $R^{-1}$ are
mutual inverses, and the hatted same-level identity in Lemma 3.2 preserves the
inverse pole condition. Therefore the required directed same-level path may
be followed from $D'$ to $D$. This proves that $D$ is reachable without using
any deficit-decreasing transition. `QED`

## Catalan Enumeration

Let $P_k$ be the number of positive Farey paths from $0$ to infinity with $k$
edges. The base edge gives $P_1=1$.

Every nontrivial positive path contains cusp $1$. Indeed, if Farey neighbors
$a/b<1<c/d$ skipped $1$, then

$$
bc-ad=b(c-d)+d(b-a)\ge b+d\ge2,
$$

contradicting determinant one. Splitting at $1$, normalize the left and right
segments by the integral determinant-one maps

$$
[0,1]\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\qquad x\longmapsto\frac{x}{1-x},
$$

and

$$
[1,\infty]\longrightarrow[0,\infty],\qquad x\longmapsto x-1.
$$

Therefore

$$
P_k=\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}P_rP_{k-r},
\qquad P_1=1,
$$

and hence

$$
P_k=\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}.
$$

The case $k=1$ consists of the two one-anchor base edges and has no both-anchor
state. For $k\geq2$, the two one-anchor classes each contribute
$\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}$. A both-anchor hatted boundary has $k+1$ edges. Its
three positive side-path edge counts are positive and sum to $k+1$, so its
count is

$$
[x^{k-2}]\operatorname{Cat}(x)^3
=\operatorname{Cat}_k-\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}.
$$

**Corollary 3 (Catalan enumeration).** Consequently Theorem 2 gives, for
$k\geq1$,

$$
|\mathcal{C}_k|
=2\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}
+\operatorname{Cat}_k-\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}
=\operatorname{Cat}_k+\operatorname{Cat}_{k-1}.
$$

Together with $\mathcal{C}_0=\{\varnothing\}$, this gives

$$
\sum_{k\ge0}|\mathcal{C}_k|x^k
=(1+x)\operatorname{Cat}(x)
=\frac{(1+x)(1-\sqrt{1-4x})}{2x}.
$$

The coefficient sequence is OEIS A167422 \cite{oeisA167422}. That cross-reference
identifies the integer sequence and its algebraic generating function; it is
not evidence for Theorem 2.

## Fibonacci Envelope

### Determinant universes

For a cusp $x$, let $v_x$ be its declared primitive integral vector. Define

$$
\mathcal{C}_{\leq k}=\bigcup_{j=0}^k\mathcal{C}_j,
\qquad
U_{\leq k}=\bigcup_{D\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq k}}\widehat D.
$$

The cumulative internal and global extrema are

$$
M_k^{\mathrm{internal}}
=\max_{D\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq k}}
\max_{x,y\in\widehat D}|\det(v_x,v_y)|,
$$

and

$$
M_k^{\mathrm{global}}
=\max_{x,y\in U_{\leq k}}|\det(v_x,v_y)|.
$$

For the one-step outgoing frontier, put

$$
G_k=\{\Phi_a(D):D\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq k},\
|\Phi_a(D)|=k+1,\ a\in\{S,R,R^{-1}\}\},
$$

$$
U_k^{\mathrm{guard}}
=U_{\leq k}\cup\bigcup_{E\in G_k}\widehat E,
\qquad
M_k^{\mathrm{guard}}
=\max_{x,y\in U_k^{\mathrm{guard}}}|\det(v_x,v_y)|.
$$

Finally let the exact-layer coordinate height be

$$
H_k=\max_{D\in\mathcal{C}_k}\max_{(n,d)=v_x,\ x\in D}
\max\{|n|,d\}.
$$

These universes are deliberately different. The $p=23$, $k=8$ hostile fixture
shows that configuration-internal injectivity does not imply global registry
injectivity, while the stabilization proof additionally needs the guard
frontier to prevent a modular collision from returning an outgoing state to
deficit at most $k$.

### Root words and typed continuant bounds

Let $V_r$ be the union of the vertex sets of all positive Farey paths from
$0$ to infinity having $r$ edges. Put

$$
P=I,\qquad N=\widetilde{R^{-1}},\qquad M=R,
\qquad L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},
\qquad Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$

so that $L(x)=x/(x+1)$ and $Q(x)=x+1$. The symbol $N$ is exactly the fixed
representative from Section 2.2; no second sign convention is introduced here.

### Lemma 5.1 (positive root coding and flag continuants)

**(i) Positive root coding.** Let $e_0=(0,1)$ and
$e_\infty=(1,0)$. Then $V_1=\{0,\infty\}$, the registries are nested, and

$$
V_{r+1}=L(V_r)\cup Q(V_r).
$$

Consequently every $u\in V_r$ has a **budget-$r$ rooted flag**
$u=w e_\varepsilon$, where $w$ is a word of length exactly $r-1$ in the
alphabet $\{L,Q\}$ and $\varepsilon$ records the chosen endpoint ray. Letters
in the terminal fan may fix the endpoint cusp while changing its flag; they
are retained when an exact budget is required. Deleting such terminal
stabilizer letters gives a minimal rooted representation of length at most
$r-1$. The cusp $1$ also has two endpoint flags. Thus the flag, rather than
the unlabelled cusp alone, retains the endpoint and padding data used by a
bridge comparison.

**(ii) Flag continuants.** Let a reduced oriented Farey flag path join two
distinct endpoint rays. Partition it into maximal fans of consecutive turns
in the same direction, with positive lengths $(q_1,\ldots,q_t)$. Set

$$
K(\varnothing)=1,\qquad K(q_1)=q_1,
$$

and

$$
K(q_1,\ldots,q_t)
=q_tK(q_1,\ldots,q_{t-1})+K(q_1,\ldots,q_{t-2}).
$$

Then the absolute determinant of the primitive endpoint rays is
$K(q_1,\ldots,q_t)$. If $m=\sum_iq_i$, then

$$
K(q_1,\ldots,q_t)\leq F_{m+1}.
$$

**Proof.** For the recursion, split any positive path at its forced vertex $1$.
A vertex on the left or right subpath pulls back under $L$ or $Q$ to a vertex
of a positive path with at most $r$ edges; nesting is obtained by inserting a
mediant into any edge. This proves the first inclusion. Conversely, apply $L$
or $Q$ to an $r$-edge path and use the base edge on the complementary side of
$1$; the resulting path has $r+1$ edges and contains the required image. The
root-word statement follows by induction from the recursion.

For the determinant identity, normalize the first oriented Farey edge to the
ordered primitive basis $(e_\infty,e_0)$. Traversing a fan of $q$ triangles
replaces the new boundary ray by $q$ times the current ray plus the preceding
ray. Thus, if $D_j$ is the determinant from the initial ray to the ray after
the $j$-th maximal fan, then

$$
D_j=q_jD_{j-1}+D_{j-2},\qquad D_{-1}=0,\quad D_0=1.
$$

This is exactly the continuant recurrence. An integral determinant-one change
of the initial oriented edge preserves the absolute endpoint determinant, so
the identity holds for every reduced flag path. Finally, induction on the last
run gives

$$
qF_{n+1}+F_n\leq F_{n+q+1};
$$

applying this inequality in the continuant recurrence proves
$K(q_1,\ldots,q_t)\leq F_{m+1}$. Alternating unit runs give equality. `QED`

### Lemma 5.2 (all-depth relative continuant budget)

Let $r,s\geq2$. For $u\in V_r$, $v\in V_s$, and
$T,U\in\{N,M,P,L\}$, choose budget-$r$ and budget-$s$ rooted flags as in
Lemma 5.1. If $\det(Tu,Uv)$ is nonzero, cancel the maximal common root segment
and then cancel every immediate backtrack in the resulting oriented flag path.
Its fan lengths $(q_1,\ldots,q_t)$ satisfy

$$
|\det(Tu,Uv)|=K(q_1,\ldots,q_t),
\qquad
\sum_iq_i\leq b_{T,U}(r,s),
$$

where

$$
b_{T,U}(r,s)=
\begin{cases}
r+s-3,&T=U,\\
r+s-1,&T\ne U,\quad T,U\in\{N,M,P\},\\
r+s-2,&\{T,U\}=\{P,L\},\\
r+s,&\{T,U\}=\{N,L\}\text{ or }\{M,L\}.
\end{cases}
$$

If the determinant vanishes, only the resulting determinant bound is asserted;
no nonzero continuant is assigned.

**Proof.** Let $\alpha$ and $\beta$ be the two budgeted positive root-flag
paths, of lengths $r-1$ and $s-1$. Terminal fan padding is chosen with the endpoint
orientation used in the bridge table. After applying the typed endpoint
transforms, the relative flag path is the concatenation

$$
\overline\alpha\; B_{T,U}\;\beta,
\qquad B_{T,U}=T^{-1}U,
$$

where the bar reverses an oriented root path. Cancelling a common root prefix
or an immediate backtrack cannot increase its length. It therefore remains to
compute only the finite path contribution of $B_{T,U}$ between the declared
endpoint flags.

With

$$
S=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$

the identities $R=SQ$ and $L=SQ^{-1}S^{-1}$ hold. Direct multiplication gives, up to
inversion and cyclic rotation of the base-triangle flag, four bridge classes:

| typed class | representative relative matrix | endpoint-flag reduction | net correction |
|---|---|---|---:|
| $T=U$ | $I$ | the two root paths meet on the same transported base edge; one terminal edge cancels | $-1$ |
| distinct $N,M,P$ | $B_{N,M}$ | one branch edge remains after the corner rotation | $+1$ |
| $P,L$ | $B_{P,L}$ | the bridge endpoint is identified with the terminal root edge | $0$ |
| $N,L$ or $M,L$ | $B_{N,L}$ or $B_{M,L}$ | two branch edges ($LQ$ or $QL$) remain | $+2$ |

Here $-1$ is a forced cancellation from the two nonzero endpoint rays, not a
path of negative length. The complete ordered matrix table is given in
Appendix A. In particular, it verifies that no fifth typed bridge class is
hidden by the flag shorthand.

Adding the two root depths to the four net corrections gives respectively

$$
r+s-3,\qquad r+s-1,\qquad r+s-2,\qquad r+s.
$$

Free reduction can only lower these values. Lemma 5.1 applied to the resulting
reduced flag path now gives both the continuant identity and the stated budget.
This argument is independent of $r$ and $s$; the finite computation is only the
four-row bridge classification. `QED`

### Lemma 5.3 (typed positive-Farey continuant envelope)

Let $F_0=0$ and $F_1=1$. For $r\geq1$, every primitive vector $(a,b)$ in
$V_r$ satisfies

$$
\max\{a,b\}\leq F_r,\qquad a+b\leq F_{r+1}.
$$

If $\gamma$ is one positive Farey path with $r$ edges, then any two vertices
$x,y$ of $\gamma$ satisfy

$$
|\det(v_x,v_y)|\leq F_r.
$$

For $r,s\geq2$, the determinant envelopes between the four typed arc registries
are bounded as follows:

| typed pair | continuant budget | determinant bound |
|---|---:|---:|
| same type among $N,M,P,L$ | $r+s-3$ | $F_{r+s-2}$ |
| distinct types among $N,M,P$ | $r+s-1$ | $F_{r+s}$ |
| $P,L$ | $r+s-2$ | $F_{r+s-1}$ |
| $N,L$ or $M,L$ | at most $r+s$ | $F_{r+s+1}$ |

The base cases involving $V_1$ are read directly from the two endpoints. The
bounds $F_{r+s}$ for $N(V_r),P(V_s)$ and $F_{r+s+1}$ for
$N(V_r),L(V_s)$ are attained by alternating Fibonacci rays.

**Proof.** The coordinate $a$, the coordinate $b$, and the sum $a+b$ are the
absolute determinants of $v=(a,b)$ with the three primitive rays of the
standard Farey triangle. A root path for $v\in V_r$ has length at most $r-1$;
the first two comparisons use that budget, while the third uses one additional
base-triangle edge. Lemma 5.1 therefore gives $a,b\leq F_r$ and
$a+b\leq F_{r+1}$.

Two vertices on one $r$-edge positive path share the part of the rooted flag
path preceding their separation. Cancelling that common part leaves at most
$r-1$ fan steps, so Lemma 5.1 gives the single-path determinant bound $F_r$.
This is not inferred merely from the two coordinate bounds.

For different typed registries, Lemma 5.2 gives the four budgets in the table;
the inequality $K\leq F_{m+1}$ gives the displayed determinant bounds. For the
sharp $N,P$ and $N,L$ rows choose the alternating rays

$$
(F_r,F_{r-1})\in V_r,\qquad
(F_{s-1},F_s),(F_s,F_{s-1})\in V_s.
$$

Their determinants are $F_{r+s}$ and $F_{r+s+1}$ by the Fibonacci addition
identities. `QED`

### Lemma 5.4 (typed registry decompositions)

For every $k\geq2$,

$$
U_{\leq k}=N(V_k)\cup M(V_{k-1})\cup P(V_k).
$$

For every $k\geq1$, the one-step guard registry is

$$
U_k^{\mathrm{guard}}
=N(V_k)\cup M(V_k)\cup P(V_k)\cup L(V_k).
$$

**Proof.** The positive registries are nested:
$$
V_1\subseteq V_2\subseteq\cdots.
$$

Indeed, inserting the mediant into any Farey edge refines an $r$-edge positive
path to an $(r+1)$-edge path without losing
an old vertex.

The three arcs of the base triangle are the images under $N$, $M$, and $P$ of
the positive base arc. In a one-anchor patch the non-base path has
at most $k$ edges. In a both-anchor patch the three positive side-path budgets
are positive and sum to at most $k+1$; hence the middle arc has budget at most
$k-1$, while the two exterior arcs have budget at most $k$. Conversely, every
vertex in these three registries occurs in a one-anchor path or in a
three-path patch after assigning the unused side budgets to base edges. This
proves the first equality.

Before same-level re-rooting, the three attach-leaf rows are:

| source rooted type | missing pole | label | budget change | new contribution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| zero-only | $-1$ | $R$ | middle $k-1\to k$ | $M(V_k)$ |
| negative-one-only | $0$ | $S$ | split at the new positive pole | $L(V_k)$ |
| negative-one-only | $0$ | $R^{-1}$ | exterior budgets stay at most $k$ | already in $N(V_k)\cup P(V_k)$ |

The first row is the carried edge $\{\infty,0\}\to\{0,-1\}$; the second and
third are the two carried-edge choices from $\{\infty,-1\}$ in Lemma 3.2.
Thus the frontier adds exactly $M(V_k)$ and $L(V_k)$ to the cumulative
registry, while $N(V_k)$ and $P(V_k)$ are already present. Taking the union
gives the guard decomposition.

The converse inclusions are constructive:

| target registry | source patch | side budgets | operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| $N(V_k)$ | one-anchor normalized patch | one non-base side $\leq k$ | same-level rooting |
| $P(V_k)$ | reflected one-anchor patch | one non-base side $\leq k$ | same-level rooting |
| $M(V_{k-1})$ | both-anchor patch | middle $k-1$, exterior $1,1$ | same-level rooting |
| guard $M(V_k)$ | zero-only source | middle $k-1\to k$ | $R$ growth row |
| guard $L(V_k)$ | negative-one-only source | positive split at pole $0$ | $S$ growth row |

For $N(V_k)$ and $P(V_k)$, choose the corresponding one-anchor positive path.
For $M(V_{k-1})$, place the path on the middle side of the base triangle and
take the two exterior side budgets to be one; the total deficit is at most $k$.
Unused budget is inserted by mediants, using the nesting of the $V_r$. The two
guard rows provide an explicit source state and label for every new $M(V_k)$ or
$L(V_k)$ cusp, while $N(V_k)$ and $P(V_k)$ are already cumulative. Thus both
displayed unions are equalities rather than only upper bounds. `QED`

### Theorem 4 (unified Fibonacci envelope)

For every $k\geq1$,

$$
H_k=F_{k+1},\qquad
M_k^{\mathrm{internal}}=F_k,\qquad
M_k^{\mathrm{global}}=F_{2k},\qquad
M_k^{\mathrm{guard}}=F_{2k+1}.
$$

The proof uses the single relative budget lemma above, rather than three
separate extremal arguments.

**Proof.** For $k=1$, the four equalities follow directly from the two base
states and their first guard frontier. Assume henceforth that $k\geq2$.
Lemma 5.3 and the first registry decomposition of Lemma 5.4 give
$H_k\leq F_{k+1}$. Equality is attained by

$$
x_k=N(F_k/F_{k-1})=-F_{k+1}/F_k.
$$

This cusp occurs in an exact deficit-$k$ one-anchor Fibonacci-comb state, so it
is a witness for the exact-layer height rather than only for the cumulative
registry.

For one-anchor states, two cusps lie on one $k$-edge positive path, so Lemma 5.3
gives the internal bound $F_k$. For a both-anchor state, two cusps on different
side paths of budgets $r,s$ satisfy $r+s\leq k$, because the third side has
positive budget. The distinct-arc clause again gives $F_k$. Equality is
attained in the positive Fibonacci comb by the pair $0,F_k/F_{k-1}$.

For the global registry, Lemma 5.4 gives exterior budgets $k,k$ and middle
budget $k-1$. The largest typed bound in Lemma 5.3 is therefore $F_{2k}$, for
the $N,P$ pair. It is attained by

$$
x_k=(-F_{k+1},F_k),\qquad y_k=(F_{k-1},F_k),
$$

because

$$
|\det(x_k,y_k)|
=F_k(F_{k+1}+F_{k-1})=F_{2k}.
$$

Finally, the guard decomposition has four budget-$k$ typed registries. Lemma
5.3 bounds every pair by $F_{2k+1}$; the only extra bridge budget is the
$N,L$ or $M,L$ case. The $N,L$ pair

$$
x_k=(-F_{k+1},F_k),\qquad z_k=(F_k,F_{k+1})
$$

attains the bound since

$$
|\det(x_k,z_k)|=F_{k+1}^2+F_k^2=F_{2k+1}.
$$

This completes the $k\geq2$ case. `QED`

## Finite-Field Stabilization

### Theorem 5 (explicit finite-field stabilization)

Set $\Phi_{a,\mathbb Q}=\Phi_a$ and
$\mathcal C_{\leq k}^{(\mathbb Q)}=\mathcal C_{\leq k}$. For
$K\in\{\mathbb Q,F_q\}$, define the labelled graph through deficit $k$ by

$$
\mathcal G_{\leq k}^{(K)}
=\left\{
D\xrightarrow{\ a\ }\Phi_{a,K}(D):
D,\Phi_{a,K}(D)\in\mathcal C_{\leq k}^{(K)},\ a\in\mathcal A
\right\}.
$$

The one-step guard frontier is not part of this target graph; it is retained
only to rule out an out-of-range rational successor folding back into the
in-range finite-field graph.

Let

$$
\Delta_k=\max\{k,M_k^{\mathrm{guard}}\}=\max\{k,F_{2k+1}\}=F_{2k+1}
\qquad(k\ge1).
$$

For every finite field $F_q$ of characteristic $p>\Delta_k$, reduction of
primitive rational cusps induces a labelled graph isomorphism

$$
\operatorname{red}_p:
\mathcal G_{\leq k}^{(\mathbb Q)}
\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }
\mathcal G_{\leq k}^{(F_q)},
$$

where the right-hand graph uses the intrinsically defined transitions from
Section 2.2. In particular,

$$
|\mathcal{C}_j^{(F_q)}|=|\mathcal{C}_j(\mathbb Q)|
\qquad(0\leq j\leq k).
$$

**Proof.** By Theorem 4, every pair of distinct cusps in the guarded registry
has nonzero determinant of absolute value at most $F_{2k+1}$. Hence reduction
modulo $p$ is injective on the entire registry, including infinity. Integral
matrix multiplication commutes with reduction, so each rational labelled edge
reduces to the corresponding finite-field edge with the same cardinality.

For surjectivity, induct along a finite-field path that stays through deficit
$k$. Lift its current state by induction. Its next rational successor either
has size at most $k$, or is an element of the one-step guard frontier and has
size $k+1$. The latter remains injective after reduction and therefore cannot
appear as an in-range finite-field state. Thus every in-range finite-field
successor has a unique rational lift. Finally, the prime-field observation in
Section 2.2 identifies the same graph inside every $F_q$ of characteristic
$p$. `QED`

## Conclusion and Open Problems

The rational defect dynamics has a finite fixed-deficit model with an exact
geometric classification. Anchored Farey patches give Catalan state counts,
and typed continuant budgets give Fibonacci arithmetic envelopes. The guarded
determinant registry supplies a concrete, if generally non-sharp, stabilization
threshold for reduction to large finite fields.

Open problems include sharp thresholds, a canonical minimal quotient of the
oriented patch transition system, uniform asymptotics in $k$, and extensions
to other arithmetic Fuchsian carriers. Route-depth and fixed-deficit limits
remain complementary rather than mutually determining.

|---|---|---|---:|---|
| $N,N$ | $I$ | terminal cancellation | $-1$ | $ad-bc$ |

| $P,P$ | $I$ | terminal cancellation | $-1$ | $ad-bc$ |
| $L,L$ | $I$ | terminal cancellation | $-1$ | $ad-bc$ |

| $M,L$ | $B_{M,L}$ | two branch edges | $+2$ | $-ac-2bc-bd$ |
| $P,L$ | $B_{P,L}$ | endpoint identified | $0$ | $ac+ad-bc$ |

The nonidentity bridge matrices are

$$
\begin{aligned}

unbounded part of the bridge calculation is the common $L/Q$ root word handled
by the continuant recurrence.

## Abstract {.unnumbered}

Consider identity-placed singleton packets on an $n$-cycle, acted on by
rotation $p$ and the adjacent idempotent $d:1\mapsto0$. Register an actual
fusion that creates a prescribed proper source arc $F$ of length $r$, and
retain its ordered parent-to-port origin, every later fusion involving its
carrier, and the complete terminal placed partition. For $n\ge3$ and
$2\le r<n$, we classify these generated summaries by a dependent bijection:
an internal origin cut, a ternary code for the selected absorption chain,
and an interval-cut and occupied-site parameter for the remaining packets.
Every parameter is realized by one continuous raw $p,d$ execution. If the
chain leaves $k$ atoms outside its carrier, its fixed-origin placement fiber
has size $n\binom{n+k-2}{k}$. Summing the fibers gives an exact formula for
the complete registered-summary set of every proper source arc. At
$r=2$ and $F=\{0,1\}$ this recovers the fixed-pair classification. For each fixed $r$,
the normalized diagonal coefficients and the unscaled count have algebraic
generating functions, and the count is asymptotic
to $\frac{4(r-1)}{3^{r+2}}n(9/2)^n$.

**Keywords:** circular packet dynamics; registered fusion; source-addressed
history; absorption chain; dependent enumeration

## 1. Introduction

A final placed partition does not say which fusion created a selected packet
or which later fusions involved it. For the circular action studied here, we
count the complete summaries obtained by recording that origin, the selected
carrier's ordered absorption chain, and the terminal placement. The selected
packet is any proper source arc, not necessarily a pair. Its two registration
parents may have different sizes, while unselected fusions can assemble
multi-atom partners and change the packets left outside the carrier. The
case $F=\{0,1\}$ was classified in Paper XLI
[@chenPaper41v1]; Section 7 identifies its exact
specialization here.

The main statement classifies *generated* summaries. Write $m=n-r$.
An origin has an internal cut
$t\in\{1,\ldots,r-1\}$; a selected chain has a code
$v\in\{0,L,R\}^{m}$; and its terminal placement belongs to a fiber
$\mathsf{Fib}_{n,m-b(v)}$, where $b(v)$ is the last nonzero checkpoint or
zero. We prove

$$
S_F\simeq
\coprod_{t=1}^{r-1}\ \coprod_{v\in\{0,L,R\}^{m}}
\mathsf{Fib}_{n,m-b(v)}.
\tag{1.1}
$$

The origin index is already visible in the smallest case beyond a pair.
Take $n=4$ and $F=\{0,1,2\}$. The chronological words
$p^3dpd$ and $dp^3dpd$ register $F$ with respective origins

$$
(d,(0,\{0\}),(1,\{1,2\}),0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
(d,(0,\{0,1\}),(1,\{2\}),0).
$$

Both end with $F$ at site $0$, $\{3\}$ at site $3$, holes at sites
$1,2$, and an empty selected chain. The placed partition alone cannot
recover which internal cut registered $F$.

The proof constructs one execution from each parameter tuple: hole slides
route packets without fusion, checkpoint words assemble and absorb the
chosen partner, and outside fusions followed by placement control reach the
specified terminal state. The inverse reads the cut, code, and placement
parameters from the complete summary. Sections 2--5 establish the
classification, Section 6 counts its fibers, and Sections 7--8 derive the
fixed-pair specialization, generating functions, and asymptotics.

The algebraic setting is adjacent to McAlister's study of semigroups
generated by a group and an idempotent, including its cyclic-group case
[@mcalister1998], and Mitchell--Levi's work on generating ranks for
endomorphisms of finite circular orders [@mitchellLevi2006].
On the combinatorial side, Mansour--Munagi enumerate set partitions by
circular successions [@mansourMunagi2014].
Those works study transformation-semigroup structure or partition
statistics. The principal contribution here is the bijection between
complete generated summaries and a dependent family of origin, chain,
and placement parameters, with realizability established by explicit
executions.

## 2. Action, Records, and Placement Control

Let $n\ge3$. The source atoms $\Omega$ and physical sites $Q$ are distinct
sets, each identified with $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ at the singleton seed.
A placed partition $P$ assigns a nonempty, disjoint atom block to each
occupied site; its blocks cover $\Omega$. Unoccupied sites are holes. The
seed is $P_\varnothing(q)=\{q\}$. The letters act on sites by

$$
p(q)=q+1,\qquad d(1)=0,\qquad d(q)=q\quad(q\ne1).
\tag{2.1}
$$

Their action on placements is pushforward union:

$$
(a_\#P)(y)=\bigcup_{q:\,a(q)=y}P(q),\qquad a\in\{p,d\},
\tag{2.2}
$$

with empty values omitted. Words are executed chronologically, left to
right. A fusion occurs precisely when $d$ sees occupied ports $0$ and $1$.
Its full record is

$$
\rho=(d,(0,U),(1,V),0,U\cup V),
\tag{2.3}
$$

where $U$ and $V$ are the actual parent blocks at those ports. In
particular, their port binding is part of the record. The registration
origin is the four-component projection of this five-component fusion record:

$$
\operatorname{Orig}(\rho)=(d,(0,U),(1,V),0).
\tag{2.3a}
$$

For $c\in\Omega$ and $1\le\ell\le n$, put
$U_{c,\ell}=\{c,c+1,\ldots,c+\ell-1\}$, with source labels modulo $n$.
Fix a proper arc

$$
F=U_{c,r},\qquad 2\le r<n,\qquad m=n-r,
\tag{2.4}
$$

and write its positive source complement as the linear list

$$
B=(c+r,c+r+1,\ldots,c+n-1).
\tag{2.5}
$$

An execution registers $F$ only when one of its actual fusions first creates
the block $F$. The origin $o_F$ is $\operatorname{Orig}(\rho)$ for that
fusion; on registered histories its omitted result is the fixed block $F$.
Thus equality of origins is equivalent to equality of the complete
registration records within $S_F$. After registration, the unique
packet containing $F$ is the *carrier*. Let $A_F(h)$ be the chronological
list of all later fusion records (2.3) in which this carrier is a parent;
fusions wholly outside it are not selected. For a registered history $h$ set

$$
\Gamma(h,F)=(P_h,F,o_F,A_F(h)),\qquad
S_F=\{\Gamma(h,F):h\text{ is registered for }F\}.
\tag{2.6}
$$

Equality in $S_F$ is equality of all four fields. Execution words and
unselected fusion records are not fields of a summary.

**Lemma 2.1 (source order).** At every generated placement, the packets are
disjoint nonempty source arcs, and their positive physical cyclic order is
their positive source cyclic order, up to rotation. This includes an arc
wrapping across the source label $n-1\to0$.

**Proof.** The seed has the asserted order. A $p$ step rotates occupied
sites without changing blocks. If $d$ sees both ports empty or only port
$0$ occupied, nothing changes. If only port $1$ is occupied, its packet
moves to the empty predecessor $0$ and passes no occupied site. If both
ports are occupied, those packets are consecutive in physical cyclic order
and hence in source cyclic order when at least three packets remain. With
exactly two packets their union is all of $\Omega$, also a source arc.
Replacing the two adjacent packets by their union preserves order.
These cases exhaust each letter, including the physical boundary
$n-1\to0$. $\square$

For a site $j$, write $[j]_n\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ for its nonnegative
representative. The chronological raw word

$$
E_j=p^{[-j]_n}\,d\,p^{[j]_n}
\tag{2.7}
$$

sends a packet at $j+1$ to $j$ at its endpoints and fixes all other sites.
If $j$ is empty, its middle $d$ sees an empty port $0$ and causes no
fusion. The rotations are also nonfusing. Thus $E_j$ is an actual
hole slide, not an additional generator. During the prefix and suffix
rotations the current placement is still obtained from the preceding
concrete placement by the indicated letter; occupancy of both ports during
a rotation does not create a fusion.

**Lemma 2.2 (placement control).** Two placements of the same named packets
in the same positive cyclic packet order are joined by a fusion-free raw
$p,d$ word. The word acts on the current concrete placement at each step.

**Proof.** With one packet, a rotation suffices even if there are holes.
Suppose there are $s\ge2$ packets $B_0,\ldots,B_{s-1}$ in their common
cyclic order. Let $h_i$ count holes after $B_i$ and before $B_{i+1}$.
If $h_i>0$, apply (2.7) at the hole just before $B_{i+1}$. It changes only

$$
(h_i,h_{i+1})\longmapsto(h_i-1,h_{i+1}+1),
\qquad(s\ge2,\ h_i>0).
\tag{2.8}
$$

Given target gaps $h_i^*$, choose an excess gap and a deficit gap and
route one hole forward along the directed gap cycle. The first donor has a
hole; each later donor has just received it. Intermediate gaps have zero
net change, so $\sum_i(h_i-h_i^*)_+$ decreases by one. Repetition reaches
the target gaps. A final power of $p$ aligns $B_0$ and consequently all
packets. If no holes exist, this last rotation already suffices.
Every slide was nonfusing on its actual predecessor placement. $\square$

**Corollary 2.3 (ordered-port admission).** Two packets consecutive in
positive source order can be placed at ports $(0,1)$ in that order, with all
other packets elsewhere, and fused by one $d$.

**Proof.** Choose such a target placement in the same cyclic packet order
and apply Lemma 2.2. The next $d$ is its sole fusion. $\square$

**Corollary 2.4 (two-packet port exception).** If exactly two named packets
$X,Y$ remain, both $(X,Y)$ and $(Y,X)$ can be placed at ports $(0,1)$ by
fusion-free words. If at least three packets remain and $X,Y$ are
consecutive in positive source order, only $(X,Y)$ is compatible with
those ordered ports while the named packets remain separate.

**Proof.** A two-packet cyclic order has both $(X,Y)$ and $(Y,X)$ as
rotations, so Lemma 2.2 admits both target placements. With at least three
packets, reversing the ports would make $Y$ immediately precede $X$ in
the physical positive order, contrary to the source order in Lemma 2.1.
$\square$

## 3. Registration Origins and a Common Boundary State

For $1\le t<r$ define the registration origin

$$
\rho_t=\bigl(d,(0,U_{c,t}),(1,U_{c+t,r-t}),0,F\bigr),
\qquad o_t=\operatorname{Orig}(\rho_t).
\tag{3.1}
$$

**Proposition 3.1 (origin classification).** The origins of $F$ are exactly
$o_1,\ldots,o_{r-1}$. Each is realized from the singleton seed by an
actual word ending at the same post-registration placement

$$
P^{\mathrm{bdry}}(0)=F,\quad
P^{\mathrm{bdry}}(i)=\varnothing\ (1\le i<r),\quad
P^{\mathrm{bdry}}(i)=\{c+i\}\ (r\le i<n).
\tag{3.2}
$$

Their post-registration summaries differ only in the recorded origin.

**Proof.** Immediately before a fusion creating $F$, its two parent
packets partition $F$ and are source arcs by Lemma 2.1. Their only
possible split is the internal cut $t$, giving the two sets in (3.1).
Since $F$ is proper, at least one outside packet is present. The source
cycle therefore reads prefix parent, suffix parent, outside packet(s).
By Corollary 2.4, the prefix must be at port $0$ and the suffix at port
$1$: reversing them would put an outside packet between those parents
in the directed order.

For realization, first apply $p^{[-c]_n}$ to the seed. In this aligned
frame atom $c+i$ sits at physical site $i$. Read the following $E$-words
in decreasing index order, omitting empty ranges:

$$
\underbrace{E_{t-2}E_{t-3}\cdots E_0}_{\text{left parent}}\quad
\underbrace{E_{r-2}E_{r-3}\cdots E_t}_{\text{right parent}}\quad
\underbrace{E_{t-1}E_{t-2}\cdots E_1}_{\text{right slide}}\quad d.
\tag{3.3}
$$

In the left loop, immediately before $E_j$, the singleton $\{c+j\}$
occupies site $j$, the already assembled interval
$U_{c+j+1,t-j-1}$ occupies $j+1$, and $j+2,\ldots,t-1$ are holes.
The middle $d$ therefore fuses precisely those packets; after $E_j$,
$U_{c+j,t-j}$ occupies $j$ and $j+1$ is empty. Descending to $j=0$
leaves $U_{c,t}$ at site $0$. The right loop has the same induction on
sites $t,\ldots,r-1$, disjoint from the left parent; it leaves
$U_{c+t,r-t}$ at site $t$. Sites $1,\ldots,t-1$ are holes. In the last
loop, immediately before $E_j$, the right parent is at $j+1$ and $j$
is empty. Each step slides it left without fusion, ending at site $1$.
The final $d$ creates $F$ with the stated port binding. All earlier
fusions occurred inside one strict parent subarc, so this is the first
creation of $F$. Outside atoms stayed singleton at sites $r,\ldots,n-1$;
the resulting placement is (3.2), independent of $t$. The cases $t=1$
and $t=r-1$ simply make the corresponding loops empty. $\square$

Call an actual registered state a **clean boundary state** if its carrier
$C$ is a packet containing $F$, its complement in positive source order
is a list of singleton packets $B$ (possibly empty), and its origin and
selected chain are fixed as $(o,A)$. Proposition 3.1 supplies one with
$C=F$ and $A=()$
for each origin.

## 4. Ternary Selected Chains

Put $\mathcal V_m=\{0,L,R\}^{\{1,\ldots,m\}}$. For $v\in\mathcal V_m$,
let $0<b_1<\cdots<b_j\le m$ be its nonzero positions and set $b_0=0$.
Starting with carrier $F$ and complement list (2.5), at checkpoint $b_i$
take $\delta_i=b_i-b_{i-1}$ atoms. If $v(b_i)=R$, take the first
$\delta_i$ atoms of the current complement and put $(C,D)$ at ports
$(0,1)$; if $v(b_i)=L$, take its last $\delta_i$ atoms and put $(D,C)$
there. Record (2.3) and replace $C$ by $C\cup D$. Let $D_F(v)$ be the
resulting ordered record list, and define $b(v)=b_j$ or $0$ if $j=0$.
The decoder is independent of the origin cut $t$: Proposition 3.1 gives
every $o_t$ the same post-registration placement, while the subsequent
record construction reads only $F$ and $v$.

**Lemma 4.1 (actual checkpoint extension).** From any clean boundary
state with nonempty singleton complement $B$, any prescribed nonempty
prefix or suffix $D$ of $B$ can be assembled outside the carrier and
absorbed in one selected fusion with the specified $R$ or $L$ port
orientation. The resulting actual state is clean, its origin is unchanged,
and its selected chain has exactly the prescribed one record appended.

**Proof.** List the atoms of $D$ in positive source order. Merge its first
two singleton packets, then that outside packet with the next singleton,
and so on. Before each merge the two named packets are source neighbors;
Corollary 2.3 places them at ordered ports along a fusion-free word. The
following $d$ merges only those outside packets. Inductively the partial
packet is the first $h$ atoms of $D$, every other outside atom remains
singleton, and neither the origin nor the selected chain changes.
There are $|D|-1$ outside fusions, or none when $|D|=1$.

Now $D$ is one packet adjacent to the carrier in the source packet cycle:
it follows $C$ for a prefix and precedes $C$ for a suffix. Use
Corollary 2.3 again to put $(C,D)$ or $(D,C)$ at ports $(0,1)$.
The next $d$ creates exactly the ordered record declared by the code.
Its new carrier is $C\cup D$; removing $D$ leaves a singleton complement
interval. Thus the resulting state is a clean boundary state with the
origin unchanged and exactly one new selected record. If $D=B$, that
complement is empty and no further checkpoint is required. If only two
packets remain, both ordered port choices are admitted by Corollary 2.4.
The routing words act successively on these placements, yielding one
continuous execution. $\square$

**Theorem 4.2 (fixed-origin chain bijection).** For each origin $o_t$,
$D_F$ maps $\mathcal V_m$ bijectively onto the attainable selected chains
with that origin. There are $3^m$ chains; exactly
$\binom{m}{j}2^j$ have $j$ selected absorptions.

**Proof.** Lemma 4.1, iterated from Proposition 3.1's actual boundary
state, realizes every code and ends after each checkpoint with the clean
condition needed by the next. Conversely, consider any registered history.
Its carrier is a source arc containing $F$, so its complement is a linear
source interval. Lemma 2.1 forces the partner in a selected fusion to be
a nonempty prefix or suffix of that interval, although outside-only
fusions may have assembled it earlier. Its full record identifies the
carrier parent and its port, hence $R$ or $L$. The cumulative absorbed
sizes $|C_i|-r$ strictly increase in $\{1,\ldots,m\}$ and recover the
nonzero positions of $v$; all other positions are zero. At the final
checkpoint, even if the carrier becomes $\Omega$, the two possible port
bindings remain distinct records. This encoding is inverse to the
construction. Choosing $j$ nonzero positions and their signs gives the
refined count. $\square$

## 5. Complete Placement Fibers and the Dependent Bijection

Fix an origin $o_t$ and code $v$. Let $C_v$ be its final carrier and put

$$
k=|\Omega\setminus C_v|=m-b(v).
\tag{5.1}
$$

When $k>0$, the outside atoms form a linear source interval. Any interval
partition of this complement may be specified by a cut set
$K\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k-1\}$. If it has $u=|K|+1$ outside blocks,
choose $u+1$ occupied sites $S\subseteq Q$ and distinguish one site
$q_C\in S$ for the carrier. Assign the outside blocks to the other sites
in positive cyclic order following $q_C$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Fib}_{n,k}
 &=\{(K,S,q_C):K\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k-1\},
             |S|=|K|+2,\ q_C\in S\},&&k>0,\\
\mathsf{Fib}_{n,0}&=Q.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.2}
$$

For $k=0$, the parameter is the sole carrier site.

**Lemma 5.1 (literal placement uniqueness).** Fix a carrier source arc
$C$ and decode $\pi\in\mathsf{Fib}_{n,k}$ into the placed partition
specified by (5.2). If two valid parameters $\pi,\pi'$ decode to the same
literal placement, then $\pi=\pi'$.

**Proof.** When $k>0$, the placement determines its support $S$, the
unique site $q_C$ occupied by $C$, and the internal source cuts $K$ between
its outside packets. These are exactly the three components of $\pi$.
When $k=0$, the sole packet is $C=\Omega$ and its site is the parameter.
Thus equality of placements gives equality of parameters in both cases.
$\square$

**Theorem 5.2 (fixed-chain placement fiber).** The summaries with fixed
$(F,o_t,D_F(v))$ are in bijection with $\mathsf{Fib}_{n,k}$. They are
exactly the placements with packet $C_v$, an interval partition of its
source complement, and the same positive cyclic packet order. In
particular,

$$
|\mathsf{Fib}_{n,k}|=n\binom{n+k-2}{k},
\tag{5.3}
$$

and, for $k>0$, the rank-$s$ part has size

$$
s\binom{k-1}{s-2}\binom{n}{s},
\qquad 2\le s\le k+1.
\tag{5.4}
$$

**Proof.** In any actual summary with the fixed chain, the unique packet
containing $F$ is $C_v$: each selected record determines its next carrier,
and a later fusion involving the carrier would add another selected record.
Every outside packet is an interval in the linear complement by Lemma 2.1.
The terminal placement therefore determines its outside cuts, occupied
sites, and carrier site uniquely, giving (5.2).

For the converse, start from the clean endpoint constructed in
Theorem 4.2. Fix the target cuts $K$. While some target outside interval
contains two current packets,
choose neighboring packets within that interval. Corollary 2.3 routes
them to ports $(0,1)$ without fusion; one $d$ merges them. Neither parent
contains $F$, so the origin and selected chain remain fixed. If $N_T$ is
the number of current packets within target interval $T$, the integer

$$
\sum_{T\text{ target outside interval}}(N_T-1)
\tag{5.5}
$$

decreases by exactly one at every such merge. It reaches zero precisely
when every target outside interval has become one packet. The carrier and
outside blocks now have the target positive source order. Lemma 2.2 moves
this actual placement to the prescribed occupied sites without fusion.
For $k=0$, rotate the sole carrier to the prescribed site. Thus every
parameter in (5.2) gives a generated summary, and the preceding readout
is its inverse by Lemma 5.1.

If $k>0$ and there are $u$ outside blocks, there are
$\binom{k-1}{u-1}$ cuts. There are $\binom{n}{u+1}$ choices of occupied
sites and $u+1$ choices of the carrier site. This gives (5.4) with
$s=u+1$. Summing and using Vandermonde's identity,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{u=1}^k(u+1)\binom{k-1}{u-1}\binom{n}{u+1}
 &=n\sum_{u=1}^k\binom{k-1}{u-1}\binom{n-1}{u}\\
 &=n\binom{n+k-2}{k}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.6}
$$

For $k=0$ there are $n$ carrier sites, agreeing with (5.3). $\square$

Define the dependent parameter set

$$
\mathcal D_{n,r}=
\coprod_{t=1}^{r-1}\ \coprod_{v\in\mathcal V_m}
\mathsf{Fib}_{n,m-b(v)}.
\tag{5.7}
$$

**Theorem 5.3 (complete generated-summary classification).** For
$n\ge3$ and $F=U_{c,r}$ with $2\le r<n$, there is a bijection

$$
S_F\simeq\mathcal D_{n,r}.
\tag{5.8}
$$

It retains the actual origin, full ordered selected chain, and terminal
placed partition together. Every parameter tuple in $\mathcal D_{n,r}$
is realized by one continuous chronological $p,d$ word from the singleton
seed.

**Proof.** Given $(t,v,\pi)$, define $\operatorname{Decode}(t,v,\pi)$ as
the complete summary with origin $o_t$, selected chain $D_F(v)$, and
terminal placement specified by $\pi$. Proposition 3.1, Lemma 4.1 and
Theorem 5.2 concatenate to realize these three fields along one raw
history, so this tuple belongs to $S_F$.

Conversely, for $\Gamma=(P,F,o,A)\in S_F$, Proposition 3.1 reads the
unique cut $t$ from $o$. Theorem 4.2 reads the unique code $v$ from the
full ordered record list $A$. The carrier is the packet of $P$ containing
$F$, and is the carrier $C_v$ decoded from those records. Theorem 5.2
reads the unique outside cut set, occupied-site set and carrier site
$\pi$, or just that site when $k=0$. Put
$\operatorname{Encode}(\Gamma)=(t,v,\pi)$.

Starting with parameters, encoding their decoded summary recovers $t$
from its ordered origin, $v$ from its full selected records, and $\pi$
from its terminal packets and sites by Lemma 5.1. Thus
$\operatorname{Encode}\circ\operatorname{Decode}=\mathrm{id}$.
Starting with a generated summary, the decoded origin and chain equal its
original fields. Its recovered cuts and sites determine the same outside
packets at the same coordinates in positive source order, so the terminal
placement agrees site by site. Hence
$\operatorname{Decode}\circ\operatorname{Encode}=\mathrm{id}$.
These equalities concern summaries. $\square$

## 6. Exact Enumeration

Let $s_{n,r}=|S_F|$ for any fixed proper source arc of length $r$.
Theorem 5.3 shows that this number is independent of its starting atom
$c$. There is one all-zero code, with $b(v)=0$. For $1\le b\le m$,
exactly $2\cdot3^{b-1}$ codes have last nonzero checkpoint $b$:
choose one of $L,R$ at $b$, choose arbitrary earlier symbols, and put
zeros later. Their respective outside sizes are $m$ and $m-b$.
Equation (5.3) therefore gives the exact total.

**Theorem 6.1 (proper-arc summary count).** For
$n\ge3$, $2\le r<n$, and $m=n-r$,

$$
\boxed{
s_{n,r}=(r-1)n\left[
\binom{n+m-2}{m}
+2\sum_{b=1}^{m}3^{b-1}
\binom{n+m-b-2}{m-b}
\right].}
\tag{6.1}
$$

**Proof.** By Theorem 5.3 and (5.3), sum the fixed-chain fibers over the
codes just counted. The $r-1$ origin components are disjoint because
origin is a field of the summary. $\square$

At the smallest complement size $m=1$ (that is, $r=n-1$), the empty
chain has $n(n-1)$ placements per origin and each of the two one-record
chains has $n$. Thus (6.1) gives
$s_{n,n-1}=(n-2)n(n+1)$, including $s_{3,2}=12$.

For a fixed origin, the chain-length refinement is immediate: exactly
$\binom{m}{j}2^j$ attainable selected chains have $j$ records. If a nonempty
chain has final checkpoint $b$ and exactly $j$ records, its multiplicity
is $2^j\binom{b-1}{j-1}$ and its fixed-chain fiber has
$n\binom{n+m-b-2}{m-b}$ placements. These refine (6.1).

## 7. Exact Relation to the Fixed-Pair Result

Let $S_n^{\mathrm{pair}}$ denote the complete registered-summary set of
Paper XLI [@chenPaper41v1]
for $F_0=\{0,1\}$, with its origin projection (2.3a), ordered selected
records, and literal terminal placement. The following identifies the
summary sets directly.

**Proposition 7.1 (fixed-pair specialization).** Set $r=2$, $c=0$, and
$n\ge3$. Then $S_{F_0}=S_n^{\mathrm{pair}}$ as sets of complete summaries.
The chain and fiber components of Theorem 5.3 specialize to Paper XLI's
Theorems 3.1 and 4.2; Theorem 6.1 specializes to its Theorem
5.1. Writing $m=n-2$,

$$
\mathcal D_{n,2}\simeq\coprod_{v\in\{0,L,R\}^{m}}
\mathsf{Fib}_{n,m-b(v)},
\tag{7.1}
$$

and

$$
s_{n,2}=n\left[\binom{2m}{m}
 +2\sum_{b=1}^{m}3^{b-1}\binom{2m-b}{m-b}\right].
\tag{7.2}
$$

**Proof.** The sole internal cut is $t=1$ and its origin is
$o_1=(d,(0,\{0\}),(1,\{1\}),0)$, the fixed-pair origin. The initial
boundary state in Proposition 3.1 is $F_0$ at site $0$ with all outside
atoms singleton, as in the fixed-pair construction. Both chain decoders
read nonzero code positions as cumulative absorbed sizes and $L/R$ as
the carrier's port; hence their complete ordered records agree. For each
code, (5.2) uses the same outside cuts, occupied-site set, and carrier
site as the fixed-pair fiber parameter (its $D,Z,q$). Thus the parameter-to-summary
maps agree as literal summary maps, though their chosen raw realization
words need not be identical. The origin index disappears from (5.7),
giving (7.1). Substituting $r=2$ into (6.1) gives (7.2), the fixed-pair
total. $\square$

For $r>2$, Proposition 3.1 gives an actual registration word for each of
the $r-1$ internal cuts, and these words reach the same post-registration
placement. From each such endpoint, Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 5.2 realize
every chain code and every parameter in its dependent placement fiber by
one continuous execution. Conversely, the ordered parents in the
complete origin recover the cut, even when two summaries share a
terminal placement. Thus the factor $r-1$ in (6.1) is a consequence of
the disjoint, actually realized components of Theorem 5.3, not a
multiplier assumed from the fixed-pair formula.

## 8. Generating Functions and Fixed-Arc Asymptotics

Theorem 6.1 first gives a generating function with $n$ held fixed.
For $n\ge2$ and formal $m\ge0$, define

$$
A_n(m)=\binom{n+m-2}{m}
 +2\sum_{b=1}^{m}3^{b-1}
     \binom{n+m-b-2}{m-b}.
\tag{8.1}
$$

Only $1\le m\le n-2$ indexes a proper registered arc, for which
$s_{n,n-m}=(n-m-1)nA_n(m)$. Values at $m=0$ or beyond this range
are formal coefficients, not additional proper-arc counts.

**Proposition 8.1 (fixed-$n$ generating function).** As a formal power
series,

$$
G_n(x):=\sum_{m\ge0}A_n(m)x^m
 =\frac{1}{(1-3x)(1-x)^{n-2}}.
\tag{8.2}
$$

**Proof.** The first term of (8.1) has generating function
$(1-x)^{-(n-1)}$. Reindexing the second term by $m-b$ gives
$2x(1-3x)^{-1}(1-x)^{-(n-1)}$. Adding them cancels one factor
$1-x$ in the numerator and yields (8.2). $\square$

To follow a fixed source-arc length while $n$ increases, set
$Z_m^{(r)}=A_{m+r}(m)$ for fixed $r\ge2$. Its $m=0$ term is the
formal coefficient $1$; terms with $m\ge1$ correspond to proper arcs.
Let

$$
t(z)=\sqrt{1-4z},\qquad q(z)=\frac{2}{1+t(z)},
\tag{8.3}
$$

with the square root expanded at $z=0$.

**Proposition 8.2 (fixed-$r$ diagonal generating function).** For every
fixed $r\ge2$,

$$
\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m^{(r)}z^m
 =q(z)^{r-2}\frac{1+t(z)}{t(z)(3t(z)-1)}.
\tag{8.4}
$$

**Proof.** The binomial-series identity

$$
\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{2k+c}{k}z^k
 =\frac{q(z)^c}{t(z)},\qquad c\ge0,
\tag{8.5}
$$

can be checked directly by formal coefficient extraction. Set $u=q-1$.
The equation $q=1+zq^2$ gives
$z=u/(1+u)^2$, $t=(1-u)/(1+u)$, and
$dz=(1-u)(1+u)^{-3}du$. Using
$[z^k]f(z)=\operatorname{Res}_{z=0}f(z)z^{-k-1}dz$ yields

$$
[z^k]\frac{q^c}{t}
 =\operatorname{Res}_{u=0}
   \frac{(1+u)^{2k+c}}{u^{k+1}}\,du
 =\binom{2k+c}{k}.
$$

The first term of (8.1), with $n=m+r$, contributes
$q^{r-2}/t$. In the tail put $j=b-1$ and $k=m-b$. Its binomial factor
becomes $\binom{2k+j+r-1}{k}$, so (8.5) and the geometric sum in $j$
give a contribution $2zq^{r-1}/[t(1-3zq)]$. Their sum is

$$
\frac{q^{r-2}}{t}\frac{1-zq}{1-3zq}
 =q^{r-2}\frac{1+t}{t(3t-1)},
\tag{8.6}
$$

because $zq=(1-t)/2$. $\square$

The preceding function generates normalized coefficients. The original
proper-arc summary counts also have an algebraic generating function.

**Corollary 8.3 (unscaled proper-arc count series).** Write
$H_r(z)=\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m^{(r)}z^m$ for the function in (8.4). For every
fixed $r\ge2$,

$$
\sum_{m\ge1}s_{m+r,r}z^m
 =(r-1)\bigl[rH_r(z)+zH_r'(z)-r\bigr].
\tag{8.7}
$$

This series is algebraic over $\mathbb Q(z)$.

**Proof.** For $m\ge1$, Theorem 6.1 gives
$s_{m+r,r}=(r-1)(m+r)Z_m^{(r)}$. The coefficient of $z^m$ in
$rH_r+zH_r'$ is $(r+m)Z_m^{(r)}$. Since $H_r(0)=1$, subtracting $r$
removes precisely the formal $m=0$ term, where the source arc would be
full rather than proper. Formula (8.4) places $H_r$ in
$\mathbb Q(z,\sqrt{1-4z})$; this field is closed under differentiation,
so the right side of (8.7) is algebraic. $\square$

**Theorem 8.4 (fixed-$r$ asymptotic with an explicit error).** For each
fixed $r\ge2$ and $n>r$,

$$
0\le 1-
\frac{s_{n,r}}{\frac{4(r-1)}{3^{r+2}}n(9/2)^n}
\le \frac{3^{r+2}}{2^{r+4}}\left(\frac89\right)^n.
\tag{8.8}
$$

Consequently,

$$
s_{n,r}=\frac{4(r-1)}{3^{r+2}}n\left(\frac92\right)^n
 \left[1+O_r\!\left(\left(\frac89\right)^n\right)\right],
\qquad
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n,r}^{1/n}=\frac92.
\tag{8.9}
$$

**Proof.** Expanding (8.2) in the opposite order gives

$$
A_n(m)=3^m\sum_{k=0}^{m}3^{-k}
               \binom{n+k-3}{k}.
\tag{8.10}
$$

The infinite sum is $(3/2)^{n-2}$. For $m=n-r$, its omitted tail is
nonnegative and, using $\binom{n+k-3}{k}\le2^{n+k-3}$, is at most

$$
2^{n-3}\sum_{k=n-r+1}^{\infty}(2/3)^k
 =3\cdot2^{n-3}(2/3)^{n-r+1}.
\tag{8.11}
$$

Dividing by $(3/2)^{n-2}$ gives the upper bound in (8.8), since
$$
(r-1)n3^{n-r}(3/2)^{n-2}
=\frac{4(r-1)}{3^{r+2}}n(9/2)^n.
$$
The limit in (8.9) follows for fixed $r$. At $r=2$, (8.8) reads
$0\le1-s_{n,2}/[n(9/2)^{n-2}]\le(8/9)^{n-2}$, recovering the
fixed-pair growth bound and determining the sign of its error. $\square$

For fixed $r$, the source-arc length changes the prefactor
$4(r-1)/3^{r+2}$, but not the exponential growth base $9/2$.

## Abstract

A terminal placed partition does not determine the mergers that produced it.
For a circular packet system generated by positive rotation and the adjacent
collapse $d(1)=0$, we characterize the complete effective-fusion logs and
terminal placements jointly attainable from any initial placement of $t$
disjoint named packets on $n$ sites. Every admissible ordered cyclic-neighbor
merger schedule and compatible literal endpoint can be jointly realized by
a single chronological word.
We give a representative of length at most $\frac72n(n-1)$ preserving the
complete log and endpoint, with explicit routing budgets. At terminal rank
$s$, the joint objects number $(t!/s!)s\binom ns$. For a specified
singleton-start all-right history, the exact minimum is
$\frac32n(n-1)-1$. An exact surplus identity decomposes every realizing
word's additional length into nonnegative rotation slack, weighted carrier
backslides, and idle defects. Zero surplus forces a unique literal
chronological word. The same endpoint alone has a linear-length realization.
Thus retaining the prescribed merger history can raise the cost of that
endpoint from linear to quadratic.

**Keywords:** circular coalescence; fusion history; transformation
semigroups; constructive normal forms; word length

## 1. Introduction

An execution of a merging system has several possible outputs. Its final
transformation records where the atoms went. Its terminal placed partition
records which atoms now travel together and where their packets are. A
complete effective-fusion log additionally records the actual ordered
parents of every merger and the order in which the mergers occurred.
Executions with the same endpoint can have different logs, and prescribing
a log can change the shortest realization substantially.

We consider the literal alphabet consisting of a positive rotation $p$ on
an $n$-cycle and one adjacent idempotent $d$, which sends site $1$ to site
$0$ and fixes all other sites. Every occurrence of either letter has unit
cost. The initial packets may be arbitrary nonempty, disjoint atom sets
at arbitrary distinct sites. Their initial physical cyclic order, rather
than a numerical order on the atoms, supplies the relevant geometry.

Our construction replays an ordered merger schedule on the placement left
by each preceding step, then aligns the endpoint by fusion-free gap flow.
Boundary deletions enumerate the resulting log-endpoint pairs. For one
all-right history, rotation and hole ledgers account for every excess
letter and force the unique shortest chronological word.

These questions are adjacent to the study of transformation semigroups
generated by a group and an idempotent
[@mcalister1998], and generating ranks for endomorphisms of finite circular
orders [@mitchellLevi2006]. Cameron, Castillo-Ramirez, Gadouleau and Mitchell
study $\ell(D,\alpha)$, the minimum number of digraph-arc generators
expressing a transformation $\alpha$ [@cameron2017, Section 1]. Each
available arc $a\to b$ is a defect-one idempotent letter of unit cost.
Here only the fixed letters $p,d$ have unit cost. For example, the
circular adjacent arc $b+1\to b$ is realized chronologically by
$p^{[-b]_n}dp^{[b]_n}$, whose expanded length is
$1+[-b]_n+[b]_n$: it is $n+1$ when $b\ne0$, and one when $b=0$.
The displayed conjugate is not an additional unit-cost letter.

Their target is an endpoint transformation. From our singleton start,
the literal endpoint determines the induced transformation on atoms,
but equality of the complete log and endpoint is strictly finer than
endpoint equality alone, as Section 7 demonstrates. More generally,
our target from any fixed packet start $P_0$ is the pair consisting
of its complete ordered fusion log and its terminal placed partition.

## 2. Placements and Complete Logs

Fix $n\ge3$ and $Q=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. Let $\Omega$ be a finite
nonempty atom set, independent of $Q$. A placed partition is a map
$P:Q\to2^\Omega$ whose nonempty values are pairwise disjoint and cover
$\Omega$. Its occupied sites and rank are

$$
\operatorname{supp}P=\{q:P(q)\ne\varnothing\},
\qquad \operatorname{rank}P=|\operatorname{supp}P|.
\tag{2.1}
$$

The nonempty values are packets, distinguished by their literal atom sets.
The site maps are

$$
p(q)=q+1,\qquad d(1)=0,\quad d(q)=q\quad(q\ne1).
\tag{2.2}
$$

They act by pushforward union:

$$
(a_\#P)(y)=\bigcup_{q:a(q)=y}P(q),\qquad a\in\{p,d\}.
\tag{2.3}
$$

Words are chronological: $uv$ means execute $u$ and then $v$. Write
$P\cdot w$ for the successor. A **fusion** occurs exactly when a $d$
step has both sites $0,1$ occupied. Its complete record is

$$
\rho=(d,0,1,U,V,0,U\cup V),\qquad U=P(0),\quad V=P(1).
\tag{2.4}
$$

The order of $U,V$ is part of the record. Let $\mathcal H_P(w)$ be the
list of all such records in chronological order. Nonfusing letters add
nothing to the list. Every fusion reduces rank by one, while every other
step preserves rank. Thus an execution from rank $t$ to rank $s$ has
exactly $t-s$ recorded events.

For an integer $a$, let $[a]_n$ be its least nonnegative residue. A power
$p^k$ denotes $k$ charged applications of $p$.

Fix an initial placement $P_0$ of rank $t$. List its packets in positive
physical cyclic order as $B_0,\ldots,B_{t-1}$, with indices understood
cyclically. A cyclic block of initial packets is a union of consecutive
$B_i$ in that order. No numerical cyclic order on $\Omega$ is required.

**Lemma 2.1 (cyclic packet order).**

In every execution from $P_0$, each packet is a cyclic block of initial
packets, and the physical cyclic order of the current packets agrees
with their induced cyclic order. Every effective merger combines ordered
cyclic neighbors. At rank two both ordered pairs are possible cyclic
neighbor pairs.

**Proof.** The assertion holds initially. A $p$ step rotates the physical
positions and changes no packet. A nonfusing $d$ either fixes the placement
or moves the packet at site $1$ into the adjacent empty site $0$. It crosses
no occupied site and preserves cyclic packet order. A fusing $d$ replaces
the packets at adjacent sites $0,1$ by their union. They are cyclic
neighbors in that ordered direction, so their union is again a cyclic
block and the remaining packet order is preserved. Induction on the word
proves the assertion. When only two packets remain, each is the next
packet after the other in the cyclic list. $\square$

An **admissible schedule** from $P_0$ is a finite sequence of records of
the form (2.4), constructed by repeatedly choosing two distinct ordered
cyclic neighbors in the current packet list and replacing them by their
union. At rank one no further merger is allowed. The initial list is the
list of packets of $P_0$. For a schedule ending at
rank $s$, a **compatible endpoint** is any placement of its final $s$
packets at distinct sites in the induced cyclic order. Admissibility and
compatibility are defined directly from this packet cycle.

## 3. Fusion-Free Routing

**Lemma 3.1 (a batch through consecutive holes).**

Suppose a packet $V$ is at site $b$, and the sites
$b-1,\ldots,b-f$ are holes, where $1\le f\le n-2$. The word

$$
\mathsf B_{b,f}=p^{[1-b]_n}d(pd)^{f-1}p^{[b-f]_n}
\tag{3.1}
$$

moves $V$ to $b-f$, restores every other packet to its original site,
and produces no fusion. Its length is at most $2n+f$.

**Proof.** The prefix sends $V$ to site $1$ and the preceding original
hole $b-1$ to site $0$. The first $d$ moves $V$ to $0$. Each subsequent
$p$ returns $V$ to site $1$, and the next $d$ moves it into the next
preceding hole. Before the $k$th $d$, the site at port $0$ corresponds to
the original site $b-k$, for $1\le k\le f$. Thus no one of these steps
fuses packets or moves another packet by $d$.

All other packets receive the total rotation

$$
[1-b]_n+(f-1)+[b-f]_n\equiv0\pmod n.
$$

They therefore return to their starting sites. The last $d$ leaves $V$
at $0$, and the suffix puts it at $b-f$. If $f\ge2$, the two outer
exponents sum to a nonnegative integer congruent to $1-f$ modulo $n$
and at most $2n-2$; hence
$[1-b]_n+[b-f]_n\le2n+1-f$. Adding the $2f-1$ intervening letters
gives length at most $2n+f$. If $f=1$, the outer exponents sum to $0$
or $n$, giving length $1$ or $n+1$. $\square$

A nonempty use of (3.1) is called one batch and accounts for $f$ unit
hole transfers.

**Lemma 3.2 (one ordered merger).**

Let $U,V$ be ordered cyclic neighbors at sites $a,b$ of a placement with
at least two packets. Set $f=[b-a-1]_n$. There is a word that produces
exactly the event $(d,0,1,U,V,0,U\cup V)$, with no earlier fusion, using
at most one batch, $f$ unit hole transfers and $3n+f$ letters.

**Proof.** All $f$ sites between $a$ and $b$ are holes. If $f>0$, apply
Lemma 3.1 to $V$; the rank assumption ensures $f\le n-2$. It moves $V$
to $a+1$ and restores every other packet, including $U$. For $f=0$ no
batch is needed. Rotate by $p^{[-a]_n}$ and execute one $d$. The parents
now occupy ports $0,1$ in the requested order, so the event is exactly the
specified tuple. The batch costs at most $2n+f$, and the final rotation
and fusion cost at most $n$. When only two packets remain, apply this
construction separately to $(U,V)$ and $(V,U)$. Both are ordered cyclic
neighbors, and the two resulting records remain distinct even though
their result sets coincide. $\square$

**Proposition 3.3 (same-order placement).**

Suppose $P,P^\star$ have the same $s$ named packets in the same cyclic
order. A fusion-free word takes $P$ to $P^\star$; when $s=1$, it is a
rotation. Its batch and hole-transfer budgets, and its letter bound, are

$$
K_{\rm place}\le s-1,\qquad
M_{\rm place}\le(n-s)(s-1),
\tag{3.2}
$$

and

$$
H(n,s)=(3n-s)(s-1)+(n-1)
\tag{3.3}
$$

**Proof.** Handle $s=1$ by rotation. For $s\ge2$, fix a cyclic naming
$C_0,\ldots,C_{s-1}$ of the packets. Let $g_i$ and $g_i^\star$ be the
numbers of holes after $C_i$ and before $C_{i+1}$ in the two placements.
Both vectors sum to $h=n-s$.

Choose nonnegative integral flows satisfying

$$
g_i^\star=g_i+f_{i-1}-f_i.
\tag{3.4}
$$

For example, form successive prefix sums of $g_i-g_i^\star$ around the
cycle and subtract their minimum. Their total increment is zero, so this
gives (3.4) and at least one zero-flow edge $b$. Moreover $f_i\le h$:
relative to an edge where the minimum occurs, $f_i$ is the difference of
the current and target gap masses on one cyclic interval, each between
$0$ and $h$.

Starting after $b$, process edges $b+1,\ldots,b+s-1$ in cyclic order.
At edge $i$ move $C_{i+1}$ backward through $f_i$ holes by Lemma 3.1,
omitting the step when $f_i=0$. The preceding transfer has added
$f_{i-1}$ holes to gap $i$; later transfers have not yet touched it.
Its current size is therefore

$$
g_i+f_{i-1}=g_i^\star+f_i\ge f_i.
\tag{3.5}
$$

Every batch is enabled on the actual placement left by its predecessor.
It decreases gap $i$ by $f_i$ and increases gap $i+1$ by that amount,
without moving another packet or creating a fusion. When the last edge
has been processed, (3.4) also fixes the zero-flow gap $b$, so all gaps
equal their target values.

There are at most $s-1$ nonzero flows and their sum is at most $h(s-1)$.
The batches cost at most $2n(s-1)+h(s-1)$. A final rotation sends $C_0$
to its prescribed site, costing at most $n-1$ letters. Equal gap vectors
then fix every other named packet at its prescribed site as well. This
proves literal placement equality and (3.2)--(3.3). If $s=n$, both gap
vectors and every normalized flow are zero, so the construction uses only
the final rotation. $\square$

## 4. Joint Realization and Quadratic Normal Form

**Theorem 4.1 (log and endpoint realization).**

Fix any initial placement $P_0$ of rank $t$, and let $1\le s\le t\le n$.
A structured list $\mathcal H$ and a placement $P^\star$ of rank $s$
are jointly attainable from $P_0$
if and only if $\mathcal H$ is an admissible schedule and $P^\star$ is
compatible with it. When these conditions hold, one word $W$ satisfies

$$
\mathcal H_{P_0}(W)=\mathcal H,\qquad P_0\cdot W=P^\star,
\tag{4.1}
$$

with

$$
\begin{aligned}
|W|\le B(n,t,s):={}&3n(t-s)+(n-t)(t-s)+\binom{t-s}{2}\\
                 &+(3n-s)(s-1)+(n-1).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.2}
$$

**Proof.** Necessity follows from Lemma 2.1 and the exact event definition.
For sufficiency, let $d=t-s$ be the number of schedule records and write
$\mathcal H=(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_d)$. Starting at $P_0$, replay the records
by Lemma 3.2. After $j$ events maintain the invariant that the actual log
is $(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_j)$ and the actual source-block partition is
the abstract schedule partition after those events. Physical positions
are not part of this induction invariant.

The next recorded parents are present by that partition equality. Their
ordered adjacency in the abstract cyclic list is also their ordered
adjacency in the actual placement, by Lemma 2.1. Lemma 3.2 therefore
applies to the current concrete placement, appending exactly the next
record and making the required union. The induction continues from the
placement reached by the preceding record.

After the $d$ events, the current and target placements have the same
packets in the same cyclic order. Proposition 3.3 restores $P^\star$
without adding an event. The concatenated word proves both equalities
in (4.1). If $t=s$, the replay stage is empty and only Proposition 3.3
is used. If $s=1$, its final stage is just the rotation of the single
packet, including the case $t=s=1$.

Initially there are $h=n-t$ holes. Before event $j+1$ there are $h+j$
holes, so its parent gap has length $f_j\le h+j$. Its route costs at
most $3n+f_j$. Thus the complete fusion stage costs at most

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}(3n+f_j)
\le3nd+hd+\binom d2.
\tag{4.3}
$$

Adding $H(n,s)$ proves (4.2), including an empty schedule. $\square$

**Corollary 4.2 (uniform bound and routing budgets).**

The word in Theorem 4.1 can be chosen with

$$
|W|\le\frac72n(n-1),\qquad
K\le t-1,
\tag{4.4}
$$

and

$$
M\le(t-s)(n-t)+\binom{t-s}{2}+(n-s)(s-1).
\tag{4.5}
$$

For a fully occupied start, $K\le n-2$ when $s<n$; when $s=n$, a rotation
of at most $n-1$ letters suffices.

**Proof.** The replay uses at most $t-s$ batches and the final placement
at most $s-1$, proving the bound on $K$. Summing the parent gaps in
(4.3) and the terminal transfer bound proves (4.5).

Define

$$
Q(n,s)=3n(n-s)+\binom{n-s}{2}+H(n,s).
$$

With $h=n-t$, expansion gives

$$
Q(n,s)-B(n,t,s)=3nh+\binom h2\ge0,
\qquad
Q(n,s+1)-Q(n,s)=-(n+s-1)<0.
\tag{4.6}
$$

It follows that $B(n,t,s)\le Q(n,1)=\frac72n(n-1)$. If $t=n$ and $s<n$,
the first merger has zero parent gap, removing one batch from the bound.
If $t=s=n$, the compatible endpoint is a rotation of the start. Sparse
zero-merger targets are covered by Proposition 3.3. $\square$

For the fully occupied start, the transfer budget also has the form

$$
M\le\binom{n-s}{2}+(n-s)(s-1)
 =\binom{n-1}{2}-\binom{s-1}{2}.
$$

The dependence of the length budget on terminal rank can equivalently be
written

$$
\frac72n(n-1)-Q(n,s)
 =\frac{(s-1)(s+2n-2)}2.
$$

These are identities for the construction's budget, not formulas for the
shortest length of each target. In the zero-fusion fully occupied case,
$K=M=0$ and the sharper length bound is $n-1$.

**Corollary 4.3 (complete effective-log compression).**

For every word $w$ from $P_0$, there is a word $W$ of length at most
$B(n,t,s)$, where $s=\operatorname{rank}(P_0\cdot w)$, with

$$
\bigl(\mathcal H_{P_0}(W),P_0\cdot W\bigr)
=\bigl(\mathcal H_{P_0}(w),P_0\cdot w\bigr).
\tag{4.7}
$$

**Proof.** Its actual log is admissible and its endpoint compatible, so
apply Theorem 4.1. $\square$

Every observation determined by the complete effective log and literal
endpoint in (4.7) is preserved.

## 5. Log Counts and Forgetful Fibres

For $t=1$ the only schedule is empty. For $t\ge2$, mark the $t$ original
directed boundaries between successive $B_i$. Until rank
one is reached, every current packet corresponds to one interval of this
initial packet circle. Merging ordered neighbors deletes the original
boundary from the left parent to the right parent.

**Lemma 5.1 (boundary-deletion encoding).**

Schedules ending at rank $s$ are in bijection with ordered lists of
$t-s$ distinct initial boundaries.

**Proof.** With at least two current intervals, each remaining directed
boundary has a distinct interval on either side. Deleting it merges those
two intervals in the boundary's order, so every ordered list of $t-s$
distinct boundaries gives a schedule.

For the inverse, retain the initial boundary labels on the current
interval circle. The complete ordered parents in the next record identify
the unique boundary directed from the last initial packet of the left
interval to the first initial packet of the right interval. Delete this
label and continue. At rank two the two possible parent orders select the
two different remaining boundaries, although both result sets are the full
atom set. Induction on the record list proves that encoding and decoding
are inverse. At rank one the last undeleted boundary is not visible in the
endpoint, but is recovered as the unique label absent from the deletion
list. The $t=s=1$ case is the unique empty list. $\square$

**Theorem 5.2 (rank-refined joint count).**

From any fixed rank-$t$ start, for $1\le s\le t$,

$$
\#\{\mathcal H:\text{terminal rank }s\}=\frac{t!}{s!},
\qquad
\#\{P^\star\mid\mathcal H\}=s\binom ns,
\tag{5.1}
$$

and therefore

$$
\#\{(\mathcal H,P^\star):\operatorname{rank}P^\star=s\}
=\frac{t!}{s!}\,s\binom ns.
\tag{5.2}
$$

**Proof.** Lemma 5.1 gives $t! /s!$ boundary lists. Fix one log and its
$s$ resulting named packets. Choose an $s$-element occupied-site set in
$\binom ns$ ways and choose which packet occupies its least site in $s$
ways. The cyclic order fixes every other packet at every other occupied
site. Conversely the literal placement recovers both choices. Theorem
4.1 realizes each choice for this very log. This proves both fibre
cardinality and the product count. For $s=1$ the occupied-site choice is
one of the $n$ phases. $\square$

More explicitly, let $\mathsf{Joint}_{P_0,s}$ consist of actual complete
logs paired with their literal endpoints, rather than execution words.
Write $\mathsf{Logs}_{t,s}$ for the admissible logs,
$\mathsf{End}(\mathcal H)$ for the literal endpoints jointly attainable
with one such log, and $\mathsf{Coord}_{n,s}$ for occupied-site and
packet-anchor choices. The argument establishes the dependent bijections

$$
\mathsf{Joint}_{P_0,s}
 \simeq\coprod_{\mathcal H\in\mathsf{Logs}_{t,s}}
                \mathsf{End}(\mathcal H)
 \simeq\mathsf{Logs}_{t,s}\times\mathsf{Coord}_{n,s}.
$$

The first map retains the log and every packet at its physical site. For
the second, fix the terminal packet cycle computed from the log: encoding
reads its occupied sites and anchor, while decoding places that same packet
cycle at those sites. Both composites recover the literal joint object.

**Proposition 5.3 (fibres over literal endpoints).**

At rank $s\ge2$ there are

$$
s\binom ns\binom ts
\tag{5.3}
$$

attainable literal endpoints. Every such endpoint admits exactly
$(t-s)!$ complete logs. At rank one there are $n$ endpoints, each
admitting $t!$ complete logs.

**Proof.** If $s\ge2$, the endpoint's source partition determines its
$s$ surviving initial boundaries. The remaining $t-s$ boundaries can be
deleted in any order; Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 4.1 make these exactly the
logs over that endpoint. Choosing the surviving boundaries gives
$\binom ts$ source partitions, and each has $s\binom ns$ placements.
At rank one no surviving boundary is identified by the full atom set.
There are $t$ choices of that boundary and $(t-1)!$ deletion orders,
giving $t!$ logs over each endpoint. This also covers $t=1$.
$\square$

The coordinate-only fibre in (5.1) forgets packet identities and has
$t!/s!$ logs; Proposition 5.3 instead fixes a literal endpoint.

For a singleton start $t=n$, summing (5.2) over rank gives

$$
n!\sum_{s=1}^n\frac{\binom ns}{(s-1)!}.
\tag{5.4}
$$

The rank-one contribution alone is $n\,n!$. Equation (5.4) counts
distinct complete logs paired with literal endpoints.

## 6. A Specified Quadratic Optimum

Now take $\Omega=Q$ and the singleton start $P_{\rm seed}(q)=\{q\}$.
Prescribe the all-right log

$$
\mathcal H_R=(\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{n-1}),\qquad
\rho_i=(d,0,1,\{0,\ldots,i-1\},\{i\},0,\{0,\ldots,i\}),
\tag{6.1}
$$

and prescribe the final packet $Q$ at site $n-1$. Let $L_R(n)$ be the
minimum length realizing this log and endpoint.

**Lemma 6.1 (an explicit realization).**

The chronological word

$$
W_n=d\left[\prod_{j=1}^{n-2}p^{n-j}d(pd)^j\right]p^{n-1}
\tag{6.2}
$$

realizes the target and has length $\frac32n(n-1)-1$.

**Proof.** The initial $d$ creates $\{0,1\}$ at $0$. Before stage $j$,
the carrier is $\{0,\ldots,j\}$ at $0$, the sites $1,\ldots,j$
are holes, and the remaining singletons occupy their original sites.
After $p^{n-j}$, singleton $j+1$ is at $1$ and site $0$ is empty.
The first $d$ slides that singleton to $0$. The next $j$ copies of
$pd$ slide it through the holes; on the last copy the carrier has
returned to $0$, so the $d$ fuses the requested ordered parents.
There are $j$ nonfusing slides and one fusion in this stage. Every other
packet receives a full $n$ rotations and no slide, so its original site
is restored. This proves the stage invariant.

After the last stage the full packet is at $0$, and the suffix puts it
at $n-1$. The stages cost $n+j+1$ letters each, so

$$
|W_n|=1+\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(n+j+1)+(n-1)
      =\frac32n(n-1)-1.
\tag{6.3}
$$

The number of rotations is $P_{\min}=n(n-1)-1$, and the number of
nonfusing slides is $T=(n-1)(n-2)/2$. $\square$

We prove optimality by accounting separately for rotations and slides.
Because (6.1) prescribes the complete fusion log, every realizing word
has exactly those $n-1$ fusions: no additional outside-only fusion is
allowed. Consequently every packet not yet absorbed into the carrier
remains its original singleton. Every other $d$ step is then an
external-singleton slide, a carrier backslide from $1$ to $0$, or a step
that leaves the placement unchanged. Write their counts as
$S,B,Z$, respectively. Let $B_m$ count carrier backslides while exactly
$m$ external singletons remain; thus $B=\sum_{m=0}^{n-2}B_m$.

**Lemma 6.2 (rotation cost of a backslide).**

For any realizing word $w$,

$$
N_p(w)\ge P_{\min}+B.
\tag{6.4}
$$

**Proof.** Before the first fusion, the placement is fully occupied, so
the prefix consists of rotations. The first ordered parents in (6.1)
force their number to be a multiple of $n$.

Between consecutive specified fusions, the carrier starts and ends at
$0$. Each rotation increments its site and each backslide decrements it,
so $N_p-B$ on that interval is a multiple of $n$. Immediately after a
fusion, and immediately after a carrier backslide, the carrier is at $0$
and site $1$ is empty. At least one rotation is needed before the next
backslide. After the last backslide, or after the preceding fusion if
there was no backslide, at least one further rotation is needed before
the next specified fusion. A $d$ cannot populate the empty site $1$.
Consequently $N_p\ge B+1$ on the interval, making the multiple of $n$
strictly positive. It follows that $N_p\ge n+B$ on each of the
$n-2$ inter-fusion intervals.

After the last fusion only the carrier remains. The same site accounting
gives $N_p-B\equiv n-1\pmod n$ on the tail. Every backslide requires a
preceding rotation, so this difference is nonnegative and hence at least
$n-1$. Adding the intervals proves (6.4). $\square$

**Lemma 6.3 (hole-potential identity).**

The external slides satisfy

$$
S=T+\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}mB_m,
\qquad T=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}2.
\tag{6.5}
$$

**Proof.** While $m$ external singletons remain, anchor their cyclic
order at the carrier and let $g_0,\ldots,g_m$ be the successive hole
gaps. In particular $g_0$ follows the carrier. Set

$$
\Phi_m(g)=\sum_{i=0}^m(m-i)g_i.
\tag{6.6}
$$

Rotations and unchanged $d$ steps preserve this potential. An external
singleton slide transfers a hole from its preceding gap to its following
gap, decreasing $\Phi_m$ by one. A carrier backslide transfers a hole
from $g_m$, whose coefficient is zero, to $g_0$, whose coefficient is
$m$, increasing the potential by $m$.

At a prescribed right fusion the gap $g_0$ is zero. After the fusion
the new gaps are $(g_1+1,g_2,\ldots,g_m)$: the vacated site $1$ adds
one hole immediately after the enlarged carrier. Therefore

$$
\Phi_{m-1}(g_1+1,g_2,\ldots,g_m)-\Phi_m(0,g_1,\ldots,g_m)=m-1.
\tag{6.7}
$$

Immediately after registration $m=n-2$, with gap vector
$(1,0,\ldots,0)$ and potential $n-2$. At rank one the potential is
zero. Summing all changes yields

$$
0=(n-2)-S+\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}mB_m
                 +\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}(m-1).
$$

The two terms independent of backslides sum to $T$, proving (6.5).
The rank-one backslides $B_0$ have no effect on this identity.
$\square$

**Theorem 6.4 (exact optimum and excess cost).**

The specified target has exact minimum

$$
L_R(n)=\frac32n(n-1)-1.
\tag{6.8}
$$

More precisely, put $R=N_p(w)-P_{\min}-B\ge0$. Every realizing word
satisfies

$$
|w|-L_R(n)
=R+2B_0+\sum_{m=1}^{n-2}(m+2)B_m+Z.
\tag{6.9}
$$

**Proof.** There are $n-1$ fusing letters, so
$|w|=N_p+(n-1)+S+B+Z$. Substitute (6.4)--(6.5) and
$N_p=P_{\min}+B+R$. The constant part is
$P_{\min}+(n-1)+T=\frac32n(n-1)-1$, and the remaining terms
give (6.9). They are all nonnegative. Lemma 6.1 attains the bound.
$\square$

Since every term in (6.9) is nonnegative, equality forces
$R=Z=B_m=0$ for every $m$. Appendix A derives the literal word from
these equalities.

**Corollary 6.5 (uniqueness of the shortest word).**

The only shortest word for this target is $W_n$ in (6.2).

Equivalently, with $\operatorname{RealizesAllR}(W)$ denoting exactly the
complete log (6.1) and the specified literal endpoint,

$$
\exists!W\in\{p,d\}^*,\qquad
\operatorname{RealizesAllR}(W)\land
 |W|=\frac{3n(n-1)}2-1.
$$

**Proof.** Appendix A shows that every zero-surplus realization equals
$W_n$ as a literal chronological word; Lemma 6.1 supplies existence.
$\square$

Let $D_n^{\rm log}$ be the maximum, over all jointly attainable complete
logs and literal endpoints from the singleton start, of their minimum
realization length. Theorem 5.2 makes this a maximum over a finite set.
Theorems 4.1 and 6.4 give

$$
\frac32n(n-1)-1\le D_n^{\rm log}\le\frac72n(n-1).
\tag{6.10}
$$

Thus $D_n^{\rm log}$ has quadratic order. The exact minimum $L_R(n)$
concerns the specified complete log and literal endpoint; $D_n^{\rm log}$
ranges over such targets. Neither quantity is the diameter of the
underlying state transition graph.

## 7. Forgetting History Can Change the Cost Order

Consider the much shorter word

$$
V_n=d(pd)^{n-2}p^{n-1}.
\tag{7.1}
$$

After the first $d$, every $pd$ brings the preceding singleton to port
$0$ and the carrier to port $1$, then fuses them. It reaches the full
packet at $0$ and finally at $n-1$. Hence it has exactly the same literal
endpoint as $W_n$, with length $3n-4$. Its ordered merger log differs
from (6.1).

Write $E_n$ for the minimum length reaching this endpoint when history
is unrestricted. A rank-$n$ start requires $n-1$ effective mergers to
reach rank one, so $n-1\le E_n\le3n-4$ and $E_n=\Theta(n)$.
Meanwhile Theorem 6.4 implies

$$
\frac{L_R(n)}{E_n}
\ge\frac{\frac32n(n-1)-1}{3n-4}
\sim\frac n2.
\tag{7.2}
$$

This is an unbounded distortion under the map that forgets the complete
log. It compares two optimization problems in the same literal alphabet
from the same source, with the same terminal placement. The quadratic
cost belongs to the prescribed chronological mergers, rather than to
that endpoint alone.

## 8. Registered Observations

A registered observation can be read from a complete log by fixing an
atom set $F$, retaining the first event whose result is exactly $F$ as
its origin, and then retaining later events in which the current packet
containing $F$ is a parent. The final placed partition supplies the current
carrier and all outside packets. This recovers the origin and ordered
selected absorption chain used in registered-summary classifications
[@chenAbsorption2026; @chenProperArc2026].

**Corollary 8.1 (compression of attainable registered summaries).**

Every registered summary attained from a singleton start has a word
realization of length at most $\frac72n(n-1)$ preserving its full
origin, selected chain and literal terminal partition.

**Proof.** Apply Corollary 4.3 to one word attaining the summary. Its
complete log and endpoint determine all the specified reads. $\square$

The corollary applies to attained summaries, including attained full-arc
registrations; it does not classify which summaries are attainable.

## 10. Conclusion

The route construction and boundary-deletion encoding yield a dependent
bijection of attainable complete logs and literal endpoints, with a
quadratic normal form. For the specified all-right target, (6.9) accounts
for every excess letter, and zero surplus forces the unique raw word
$W_n$. Forgetting the log permits the same endpoint in linear length.

## Appendix A: Forcing the Unique Shortest Word

### A.1 Activity and Exact Rotation Budgets

Call a defect letter **active** when its predecessor has port $1$ occupied.
If port $1$ is empty, $d$ leaves the full execution state unchanged and
appends no effective event. Deleting that letter therefore preserves the
complete log and literal endpoint. A minimum-length realization cannot
contain such a letter: otherwise deletion contradicts the raw lower bound
in Theorem 6.4.

Let $w$ attain equality in (6.8). By (6.9), every $B_m$, $R$ and $Z$
vanishes, so $N_p(w)=P_{\min}$. Every actual inter-fusion interval
contributes at least $n$ rotations, and the tail at least $n-1$, by
Lemma 6.2. These lower bounds already sum to $P_{\min}$. Consequently
the registration prefix has no
rotations, every inter-fusion interval has exactly $n$, and the tail has
exactly $n-1$. Before the first fusion a fully occupied placement permits
no nonfusing $d$, so registration is the literal first letter $d$.

### A.2 Local Stage Rigidity

Let $1\le j\le n-2$. Suppose a stage begins with the actual placement

$$
C_j=\{0,\ldots,j\}\text{ at }0,\quad
1,\ldots,j\text{ empty},\quad
\{i\}\text{ at }i\quad(j+1\le i<n).
\tag{A.1}
$$

Assume that its next effective fusion has ordered parents
$(C_j,\{j+1\})$, its prefix before that fusion is fusion-free, every
$d$ in the stage is active, and it uses exactly $n$ rotations. Then the
entire stage, including the fusion letter, is

$$
p^{n-j}d(pd)^j.
\tag{A.2}
$$

**Proof.** There is a defect before the final fusion: without one the
$n$ rotations restore (A.1), where port $1$ is empty. Before the first
defect only rotations have occurred. That defect is active and nonfusing,
so port $0$ is a hole and port $1$ occupied. The only hole-to-packet
boundary of (A.1) in the positive direction ends at singleton $j+1$.
Among rotation counts from $0$ to $n$, its ordered placement at ports
$0,1$ occurs exactly at $n-j$. This identifies the first defect as a
slide of that singleton. The calculation is cyclic and includes the
boundary crossing $n-1\to0$.

After this slide mark atom $j+1$. It is at port $0$, with exactly $j$
rotations remaining. Every other atom is at an integer representative
$k$ satisfying $2\le k\le n-j$: the carrier is at $n-j$, and all other
outside singletons are between $2$ and $n-j-1$. During the remaining
rotations their representatives stay in $[2,n]$, so none can reach port
$1$. A defect fixes all these atoms. Induction on the actual remaining
letters therefore proves that every active defect moves the marked atom
from $1$ to $0$; this is not an assumed slide ordering.

Lift the marked position to height $n$ immediately after the first slide.
Each remaining $p$ raises the height by one, and each active $d$ lowers
it by one when its residue is $1$. The height cannot fall below $n$ and
cannot reach $2n$, since fewer than $n$ rotations remain. The prescribed
final fusion places the marked atom at $0$, so its terminal height is
exactly $n$. Thus the remaining $j$ rotations are paired with exactly
$j$ marked defects. Starting at height $n$, the next defect requires
one rotation. Two rotations before it would pass height $n+1$, and the
remaining budget cannot reach another height with residue $1$. Hence
each rotation must be followed immediately by $d$, returning the height
to $n$. Repeating forces the suffix $(pd)^j$ and proves (A.2).
$\square$

### A.3 Induction over the Remaining Actual Log

After registration, the actual placement is $C_1$ in (A.1). Assume
inductively that the next unprocessed stage begins at $C_j$. Cut the
remaining raw word immediately after its next effective fusion. The
complete log (6.1) fixes the ordered parents of that event, and no
earlier fusion occurs in the extracted stage. By A.1 every intervening
defect is active. Lemma 6.2 gives at least $n$ rotations in this
interval, the same bound for each later inter-fusion interval, and at
least $n-1$ for the final tail. Since the entire word contains exactly
$P_{\min}=n(n-2)+(n-1)$ rotations, equality holds in every separate
bound. In particular, the current stage contains exactly $n$ rotations.

The local argument A.2 now forces its entire literal word to be
$p^{n-j}d(pd)^j$. Lemma 6.1 evaluates this word on $C_j$ and gives
precisely $C_{j+1}$, together with the next prescribed log event. This
re-establishes the induction invariant on the actual execution left by
the stage. Iterating to $j=n-2$ fixes all inter-fusion stages.

### A.4 The Rank-One Tail and Unique Existence

After the last fusion the full packet is at $0$. All defects in the
remaining tail are active, so each would be a carrier return from $1$
to $0$. Starting at lifted height zero, the $n-1$ rotations give
terminal height at most $n-1$. Its prescribed residue $n-1$ forces
that height to equal $n-1$, leaving no downward step. Therefore the
tail contains no $d$ and is exactly $p^{n-1}$. This argument includes
rank-one backslides even though their hole-potential weight is zero.

Combining registration, A.3 and the tail proves literal equality $w=W_n$.
Together with the actual execution and length of $W_n$, this gives the
unique-existence statement of Corollary 6.5.

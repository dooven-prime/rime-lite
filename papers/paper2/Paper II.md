## Abstract

**Problem.** A spectral sectorization need not be preserved by the operators
whose transport it is used to study. The resulting off-diagonal blocks define
a direct support graph. However, the relations among projector non-invariance,
physical-block support, and the computed graph structure must be stated without
assuming that the sector projectors lie in a group commutant.

**Approach.** We first establish an exact finite-dimensional identity relating
off-diagonal transport to projector non-invariance, together with a separate
block-locality theorem. The computational layer registers the Rubik sectors,
direct blocks, support localizer, and finite EP algebra without promoting
graph paths to matrix compositions.

**Results.** For a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, a complete
orthogonal sectorization $I=\sum_\alpha Q_\alpha$, and a unitary operator $U$,

$$
\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\|Q_\beta UQ_\alpha\|_F^2
=\|(I-Q_\alpha)UQ_\alpha\|_F^2
=\frac12\|[U,Q_\alpha]\|_F^2.
$$

Thus off-diagonal transport is exactly the Frobenius mass of sector
non-invariance. A separate block-locality theorem shows that direct transport
vanishes between sectors with disjoint support whenever both the transport
family and the sector projectors preserve the declared physical blocks.

**Computational case study.** In the declared complex128 Rubik realization, the quarter-turn
and half-turn averages register nine numerical joint-spectral sectors. The 18
face-turn matrices produce a symmetric ten-edge direct graph. Nine certified
edges have shared thresholded noncommutative block support; the remaining
S8--S9 edge is carried by the numerically commuting corner-permutation block.
The block-level noncommutative-support test serves as a localizer rather than a
sufficient transport criterion: it selects 15 candidate pairs, of which six
are nonedges.
The computed EP averaging algebra
$A_{\mathrm{EP}}\cong M_2(\mathbb C)^4\oplus M_1(\mathbb C)^4$ supplies a
finite algebraic description of the dominant noncommutative block, but the
direct matrices $Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha$ remain the transport certificate.

**Boundary.** Exact finite-dimensional statements, numerical Rubik
registration, and mechanism labels are kept separate. Generator-family field
censuses, negative controls, and exhaustive auxiliary tables are reported in
supplementary material rather than presented as theorem claims. Graph paths are
not interpreted as matrix compositions in this paper.

## Introduction

Let $V=\bigoplus_\alpha V_\alpha$ be an orthogonal sectorization with
projectors $Q_\alpha$. Given a unitary family $\{\rho(g)\}$, the basic direct
transport object is not an abstract edge but the matrix block

$$
T_{\beta\alpha}(g)=Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha.
$$

Its nonzero pattern depends on both the sectorization and the declared
operator family. It cannot be inferred from sector dimensions, eigenvalue
proximity, or block overlap alone. It also does not require the projectors to
commute with the transport action. In fact, nontrivial off-diagonal transport
requires precisely the opposite: at least one sector projector must fail to
commute with a declared transport operator.

Throughout this paper, **transport topology** means the finite direct-support
pattern induced by the declared operator blocks, not a topology in the
point-set sense.

This paper has two layers.

1. The exact layer defines direct transport for an arbitrary complete
   orthogonal sectorization and proves the Transport--Non-Invariance Identity
   alongside a block-locality theorem.
2. The computational layer registers a nine-sector Rubik realization and
   reports its direct graph, a thresholded noncommutative-support localizer,
   the corner-permutation exception, and the corresponding EP algebra census.

The claim boundary is deliberate. Machine-zero commutators do not prove exact
commutation. The nine registered Rubik projectors are computational objects
unless an exact joint-spectrum certificate is supplied. The Type I/II labels
classify already-certified direct edges; they are not universal sufficient
conditions for transport. A separate analysis \cite{paper3} studies whether
paths in a support graph survive as projected matrix products.

The main contribution therefore has two parts: direct off-diagonal mass is
exactly sector non-invariance, while the Rubik algebraic diagnostics describe
how that mass is distributed in one finite registered system. The certified
direct blocks and edge labels can serve as inputs to routed-composition audits,
but do not themselves promote a graph path to a nonzero product.

## Spectral and Joint-Spectral Sectors

### Rubik representation and averaging families

The registered Rubik representation is block diagonal on

$$
V
=V_{\mathrm{cp}}\oplus V_{\mathrm{ep}}
 \oplus V_{\mathrm{co}}\oplus V_{\mathrm{eo}},
$$

with dimensions $64+144+8+12=228$, respectively. The generator set $S=S^{-1}$
contains the 18 standard face turns. Every $\rho(g)$ preserves these four
physical blocks.

Separate the 12 quarter turns and six half turns and define their uniform
averages $\mathrm{QT}_{\mathrm{all}}$ and $\mathrm{HT}_{\mathrm{all}}$. By
construction,

$$
A_{18}
=\frac1{18}\sum_{g\in S}\rho(g)
=\frac23\mathrm{QT}_{\mathrm{all}}
 +\frac13\mathrm{HT}_{\mathrm{all}}.
$$

The $A_{18}$-spectral projectors

$$
P_\lambda^A=\mathbf 1_{\{\lambda\}}(A_{18})
$$

and the QH joint-spectral projectors discussed below are different objects.
The former yield six coarse averaging layers in the canonical computation. The
latter resolve those layers into nine sectors and are the projectors used for
transport.

### Exact commuting model

Suppose $Q$ and $H$ are commuting Hermitian operators with spectral
projectors $E_q^Q$ and $E_h^H$. For every nonzero joint eigenspace define

$$
Q_\alpha=E_{q_\alpha}^QE_{h_\alpha}^H,
\qquad
V_\alpha^{QH}=\operatorname{im}Q_\alpha.
$$

The $Q_\alpha$ form a complete orthogonal sectorization. For an affine
combination $A=aQ+bH$, its spectral projectors are collision quotients:

$$
P_\lambda^A
=\sum_{\alpha:\,a q_\alpha+b h_\alpha=\lambda}Q_\alpha.
$$

This mathematical statement is exact under the stated commuting-Hermitian
hypotheses.
It does not assert that a numerically registered pair commutes exactly.

## Direct Transport and Sector Non-Invariance

### Definitions

**Definition 3.1 (Sectorized transport datum).** Let $V$ be a
finite-dimensional Hilbert space, let $\rho:G\to U(V)$ be unitary, let
$S\subseteq G$, and let

$$
I=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal I}Q_\alpha,
\qquad
Q_\alpha Q_\beta=\delta_{\alpha\beta}Q_\alpha
$$

be a complete orthogonal sectorization. No assumption
$Q_\alpha\in\operatorname{End}_G(V)$ is made.

**Definition 3.2 (Direct transport).** For source $\alpha$, target $\beta$,
and $g\in S$, define

$$
T_{\beta\alpha}(g)=Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha,
\qquad
K^S_{\beta\alpha}=\max_{g\in S}\|T_{\beta\alpha}(g)\|_F.
$$

Exact direct support is
$\mathbf 1[K^S_{\beta\alpha}>0]$. A numerical graph replaces zero by a
declared threshold $\tau_K$. If $S=S^{-1}$, then unitarity and Frobenius-norm
invariance give $K^S_{\beta\alpha}=K^S_{\alpha\beta}$.

### Transport--Non-Invariance Identity

**Proposition 3.3 (Off-Diagonal Transport--Non-Invariance Identity).**
\label{prop:transport-noninvariance} For every unitary $U$ and every sector
projector $Q_\alpha$,

$$
\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\|Q_\beta UQ_\alpha\|_F^2
=\|(I-Q_\alpha)UQ_\alpha\|_F^2
=\|[U,Q_\alpha]Q_\alpha\|_F^2
=\frac12\|[U,Q_\alpha]\|_F^2.
$$

**Proof.** Relative to
$V=V_\alpha\oplus V_\alpha^\perp$, write

$$
U=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\C&D\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Q_\alpha=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Then

$$
[U,Q_\alpha]
=\begin{pmatrix}0&-B\\C&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
[U,Q_\alpha]Q_\alpha
=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\C&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Orthogonality of $I-Q_\alpha=\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}Q_\beta$ gives

$$
\|C\|_F^2
=\|(I-Q_\alpha)UQ_\alpha\|_F^2
=\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\|Q_\beta UQ_\alpha\|_F^2.
$$

Unitarity implies $A^*A+C^*C=I$ and $AA^*+BB^*=I$. Taking traces yields
$\|B\|_F^2=\|C\|_F^2$. Therefore,
$\|[U,Q_\alpha]\|_F^2=\|B\|_F^2+\|C\|_F^2=2\|C\|_F^2$.
$\square$

**Corollary 3.4 (Sector invariance).** \label{cor:paper2-sector-invariance}
If $S=S^{-1}$ generates $G$, then the
following are equivalent:

1. $Q_\alpha$ commutes with $\rho(G)$;
2. $V_\alpha$ is $G$-invariant;
3. $Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha=0$ for all $g\in S$ and $\beta\ne\alpha$;
4. the outgoing off-diagonal mass in
   Proposition~\ref{prop:transport-noninvariance} is zero for every
   $g\in S$.

If $Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha\ne0$ with $\alpha\ne\beta$, then neither endpoint
projector commutes with $\rho(g)$. Indeed, commutation of $Q_\alpha$ would give

$$
Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha=Q_\beta Q_\alpha\rho(g)=0,
$$

and commutation of $Q_\beta$ would give

$$
Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha=\rho(g)Q_\beta Q_\alpha=0.
$$

### Block locality

**Theorem 3.5 (Direct Block-Locality).** \label{thm:paper2-block-locality} Let
$I=\sum_b\Pi_b$ be an orthogonal physical-block decomposition. Suppose every
$\rho(g)$ and every $Q_\alpha$ commutes with every $\Pi_b$. If

$$
\operatorname{BlockSupp}(Q_\alpha)
\cap\operatorname{BlockSupp}(Q_\beta)=\varnothing,
$$

then $Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha=0$ for every $g$.

**Proof.** Insert $I=\sum_b\Pi_b$ and use block preservation:

$$
Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha
=\sum_b Q_\beta\Pi_b\rho(g)\Pi_bQ_\alpha.
$$

For each $b$, disjoint block support makes at least one endpoint factor zero.
$\square$

This theorem is only a zero criterion. Shared physical-block support does not
imply nonzero direct transport.

### Noncommutative support is a localizer

Let $\mathrm{QT}^a$, $a\in\{0,1,2\}$, be the three per-axis quarter-turn
averages. For the declared threshold define

$$
\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathrm{nc}}(\alpha)
=\left\{
b:
\Pi_bQ_\alpha\ne0,
\ \max_{0\le a<c\le2}
\|\Pi_b[\mathrm{QT}^a,\mathrm{QT}^c]\Pi_b\|_F
>\tau_{\mathrm{nc}}
\right\}.
$$

In the canonical audit, all three axis pairs $(0,1),(0,2),(1,2)$ give the same
block norms to the displayed precision:

| Block | Norm | Ratio to total norm |
|-------|-----:|--------------------:|
| cp | $<10^{-14}$ | 0% to reported precision |
| ep | 2.7386 | 93.9% |
| co | 0.6124 | 21.0% |
| eo | 0.7906 | 27.1% |

The ratios are not additive; Frobenius masses add in quadrature. The support
sets are

| Sector | $\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathrm{nc}}$ |
|--------|---------------------------------------|
| S1 | $\varnothing$ |
| S2 | $\{\mathrm{eo}\}$ |
| S3 | $\{\mathrm{ep},\mathrm{eo}\}$ |
| S4 | $\{\mathrm{ep},\mathrm{co}\}$ |
| S5 | $\{\mathrm{eo}\}$ |
| S6 | $\{\mathrm{ep},\mathrm{eo}\}$ |
| S7 | $\{\mathrm{ep},\mathrm{co},\mathrm{eo}\}$ |
| S8 | $\varnothing$ |
| S9 | $\{\mathrm{co}\}$ |

All nine non-CP direct edges have nonempty support intersection. However, such
an intersection occurs for 15 unordered pairs in total. The six false-positive
candidates are

$$
\{\mathrm{S2--S3},\mathrm{S2--S7},\mathrm{S3--S4},
\mathrm{S3--S5},\mathrm{S4--S7},\mathrm{S5--S7}\}.
$$

By definition of the registered Type I label, every Type I edge has shared
block-level $\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathrm{nc}}$. The nontrivial empirical
statement is instead

$$
15\text{ overlap candidates}
=9\text{ certified Type I edges}+6\text{ nonedges}.
$$

Thus the support is a candidate localizer and mechanism descriptor, not a
transport certificate. Only $Q_\beta\rho(g)Q_\alpha$ or the equivalent leakage
mass certifies a direct edge.

### Type I and Type II are registered edge labels

The terminology is defined after direct-edge certification:

- **Type I:** a certified direct edge whose endpoints share thresholded
  noncommutative block support. There are nine such edges.
- **Type II:** a certified direct edge without such an intersection, carried
  here by a shared block whose per-axis QT commutator is below threshold.
  The unique instance is S8--S9 on CP.

This is an exhaustive finite classification of the ten registered edges, not
a universal dichotomy for arbitrary representations.

The S8--S9 channel illustrates why averaged commutativity and generator
commutativity must be separated. On CP,

$$
\max_{0\le a<c\le2}
\|\Pi_{\mathrm{cp}}[\mathrm{QT}^a,\mathrm{QT}^c]
\Pi_{\mathrm{cp}}\|_F<10^{-14},
$$

yet the individual CP permutation matrices need not preserve either QH
eigenspace. The directly evaluated block
$Q_9\rho(g)Q_8$ is nonzero. Thus a commuting averaging family can define
sectors that are mixed by its unaveraged generators.

### EP algebra census

The EP block carries most of the per-axis QT commutator norm. The three
registered per-axis QT restrictions are Hermitian and generate a numerically
closed 20-dimensional unital $*$-algebra with an eight-dimensional center.
Finite-dimensional unital $*$-subalgebras of a matrix algebra are
finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebras and hence semisimple
\cite{murphy1990cstar}. The computational audit reports generator Hermiticity,
identity-in-algebra,
multiplication-closure, and $*$-closure residuals of $0$,
$6.989\times10^{-15}$, $2.681\times10^{-15}$, and
$1.416\times10^{-14}$, respectively. The finite numerical Artin--Wedderburn
census is

$$
A_{\mathrm{EP}}
\cong M_2(\mathbb C)^4\oplus M_1(\mathbb C)^4.
$$

Each of the four $M_2$ simple components occurs with representation
multiplicity 12 and therefore occupies a 24-dimensional central component.
Similarly, the four $M_1$ components each have multiplicity 12 and occupy
12-dimensional components:

$$
4(2\cdot12)+4(1\cdot12)=144.
$$

Three of the four registered 24-dimensional components have nonzero
family-level commutator norm; the fourth and all four 12-dimensional
components are commuting to the declared tolerance. This algebra localizes the
dominant EP noncommutativity and provides coordinates for the finite Rubik
analysis.

It does not prove the direct graph, force a unique hub, or identify components
of the full $G$-commutant. The graph and hub degree are computed from the
transport blocks. Detailed center, Killing-form, and double-commutant tables
are reported separately and are not used in the proof of the graph census.

### Graph/composition boundary

Some length-two support paths have endpoints on disjoint physical blocks. Such
graph paths do not imply nonzero projected matrix products. For five canonical
pairs, a separate composition audit reports machine-zero ordered products with
image--kernel and block-support explanations \cite{paper3}. These graph-only
pairs are not composition morphisms, and the composition claims are not used
to establish the direct graph.

## QH Refinement Boundary

**Proposition 5.1 (Atoms of the declared QH joint spectral resolution).** If
$\mathrm{QT}_{\mathrm{all}}$ and $\mathrm{HT}_{\mathrm{all}}$ are Hermitian
and commute exactly,
then the $Q_\alpha$ are the primitive spectral idempotents of the represented
commutative algebra

$$
\mathcal B_{\mathrm{QH}}
=\operatorname{alg}(\mathrm{QT}_{\mathrm{all}},
\mathrm{HT}_{\mathrm{all}}).
$$

Equivalently, no spectral projector of an element of
$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{QH}}$ further splits a range $Q_\alpha V$.

**Proof.** Commuting Hermitian operators admit a simultaneous orthogonal
spectral resolution. Every element of the finite-dimensional commutative
algebra is a polynomial in the declared generators and acts by a scalar on
each common joint eigenspace. Its spectral projectors are sums of the
primitive joint spectral idempotents $Q_\alpha$. $\square$

This statement concerns spectral idempotents within the represented
commutative algebra. It does not imply that $Q_\alpha V$ is a minimal invariant
subspace as an abstract module: because the algebra acts on this space by
scalars, every subspace is invariant. Nor does it claim that the nine
registered Rubik sectors constitute the finest orthogonal sectorization in
$\operatorname{End}(V)$.

The per-axis averages cannot all be adjoined to the same commuting spectral
family: the registered norms satisfy

$$
\|[\mathrm{QT}^0,\mathrm{QT}^1]\|_F
=\|[\mathrm{QT}^0,\mathrm{QT}^2]\|_F
=\|[\mathrm{QT}^1,\mathrm{QT}^2]\|_F
=2.9155.
$$

This is an obstruction to simultaneous diagonalization of those particular
operators. It is not a theorem excluding every larger commuting extension of
$\mathcal B_{\mathrm{QH}}$.

## Discussion

The exact Transport--Non-Invariance Identity is independent of Rubik
structure. Whenever a unitary family is expressed relative to a complete
orthogonal sectorization, the total outgoing off-diagonal mass from a sector
equals one half of the projector-commutator mass. This identity gives a
complete generator-level answer to whether a sector is invariant.

The Rubik realization provides a finite computational case study. Its QH
projectors are registered from a numerically commuting averaging pair but are
not preserved by the full face-turn action. The resulting transport graph is
sparse and block-local. Per-axis noncommutativity localizes all nine non-CP
edges but does not determine them. The CP edge shows that a commuting averaged
block may still support generator transport. The EP algebra explains where
most of the averaged noncommutativity resides without replacing direct matrix
evaluation.

### Promotion limits

Two promotion problems remain open. First, a general
algebraic criterion for direct transport needs more than block-level
noncommutative support. Second, a graph path requires a separate product audit
before it can be interpreted as projected-composition reachability.

## Conclusion

For a complete orthogonal sectorization and unitary transport operator,

$$
\boxed{
\sum_{\beta\ne\alpha}\|Q_\beta UQ_\alpha\|_F^2
=\frac12\|[U,Q_\alpha]\|_F^2
}.
$$

The central identity equates off-diagonal transport mass with sector
non-invariance. The registered Rubik case has ten computed direct edges. Nine
have nonempty $\operatorname{Supp}_{\mathrm{nc}}$ intersection, while one uses
the commuting CP block. This intersection is part of the Type I label
definition; the registered data show that it is not sufficient for an edge.
The computed transport matrices therefore remain the certificate. This
separates block locality, mechanism labels, and composition boundaries without
assuming $Q_\alpha\in\operatorname{End}_G(V)$.

## Related Work and Novelty Boundary

Finite-group representations and matrix blocks provide the ambient language
\cite{serre1977,curtisReiner1962}. Finite-dimensional $C^*$-algebra structure
and Artin--Wedderburn theory supply the semisimple algebra background
\cite{murphy1990cstar,lam2001}.
Random-walk averages on finite groups supply the averaging-operator viewpoint
\cite{diaconis1988}.

The commuting spectral side is related to Bose--Mesner algebras and quotient
constructions \cite{bannaiIto1984,godsil1993,godsilMartin1995quotients}.
Those theories organize commuting averages and their eigenspaces. The direct
blocks studied here are additional data: they measure a separate operator
family relative to that spectral decomposition.

Related work studies the canonical averaging spectrum and projected
composition obstructions \cite{paper1,paper3}. Extended finite censuses are
available as optional supplementary data.
